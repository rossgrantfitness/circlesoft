**How it stays tight:**
- Every setting is a story beat that's already approved. Curfew (6) and the compound (7, 9) were already in the approved Act 1 at about 40 and 30 minutes; here they're split by what you do, not stretched.
- Three of the ten are re-dresses or reuses, not new builds: curfew is the slums re-dressed, the compound is one build used twice (front half and back half), and the dead ore line is the train kit again. So Harrow feels like ten places while Ross builds about seven.
- The pacing swings between short set pieces (5, 10, 12) and longer playable stretches (6, 8, 9), so no two long ones sit back to back except 8 and 9, which are split by Tilly's betrayal.
- If Act 1 runs long in playtests, trim curfew's side jobs first. Never cut a setting.

## Borrowed, openly

- **FF7's train into Midgar: the opening.** Arriving in the city on a night train, in the middle of the action. **How ours differs:** Red is alone, a courier on a smuggling run (not a hired gun on a sabotage mission); it's an ore freight with cargo, not a passenger train; the goal is to get home in time to light a lamp; and she loses the cargo, which is what kicks off the story.
- **FF7's ID-scan train run, the car-by-car sweep:** this is a second FF7 beat, so watch the stacking. Our surface: inspectors with hand scanners walking toward you, hiding behind ore, ditching the crate. The feel of being hunted on the train comes from **MGS1's guards and vision cones**, not from a timer.
- **FF7's Sector 7 slums and a Mako reactor: Harrow and the jammer works.** **Stacking check:** train → slums → a plant with keycards is the order of FF7's opening hour. Each piece is one borrowed role, and the plot inside is ours: no bombing, no reactor blast, no mercenary crew. The boss is a petty sergeant on a jammer walker, the plant is wrapped around an ancient shrine-mast, and the cards are all the same photo of Kasp. I think it passes, but this is the most FF7 the slice has ever been, so it's Ross's call (Choice 2, option B is the less-FF7 version).
- **Blade Runner and Akira: the slum look** (neon over grime, stacked streets), per the tone guide.
- **Half-Life 2's canals: the Spillway.** A drain route under an occupied city, and the way around the checkpoints.
- **Wobble Station, Slipway Prime:** unchanged (approved).
- **Pit Nine: the company town** (the mining towns of Outland and Total Recall's Mars colony: a company store and paying for air). **Breaker's Ring: the scrapyard city** (Battle Angel Alita's Scrapyard, Cowboy Bebop's junk-shop ports).

## Story check

- **"On the run" fits Red.** She runs the odd Coldrunner crate (approved), and a smuggling run gone wrong is the most Red way to meet Kasp. It also hands her signature gesture, "Already running", a literal first scene. Losing Watch Zero's crate gives her a reason to go to the docks before anyone asks. Then Otis's dockhands get shoved, and she's swinging before he finishes asking, as approved.
- **Kept:** the twist, the Keeper, the villain ladder, the climb (on foot → big robot → giant robot → giant spaceship → god), Dee, the cycle-breaking core, every approved Act 1–3 beat (beat 1 gains the train; beat 7's tunnels become the ore line; Act 2 beat 2's moons get identities).
- **All five slice clues stay put:** Otis's bridge lamp (H8); the old Zero's "gran's gran" line at the sit-in (now the Works gate); the plaque and the mural in T0 (the untouched base of the mast); Kasp's "genuine antique" dish and the beacon part on the roof. The Works being built around something older than the Hegemony makes the T0 clue land harder.
- **Fits the grim tone pass:** quota boards, a drone factory and a company mining moon are oppression you can walk through.

## Choices for Ross

DECISION NEEDED: How does the game open?
Option A: **A playable ore-train run** (~4 min): car by car past Signals inspectors, one tutorial fight, Red ditches the crate and jumps, then runs home to light her lamp — pros: starts on the run, in motion, with the controls and Clutch taught on the train; it's what your note asked for / cons: one new kit (train car) and about 2 extra minutes in the slice.
Option B: **A short train cutscene** (~1 min): Red jumps off the train with the crate and lands in the slums; play starts in Harrow — pros: cheapest (one car, no new enemy behavior), keeps the slice at ~31 min / cons: you watch the train instead of playing it, so it barely counts as "on the run".
Option C: **Start mid-heist in the jammer works, then flash back to the train** — pros: the boldest opening / cons: flashbacks in a 30-minute slice confuse the order, and it spoils the climb.
Recommendation: A. It's the opening you described, and the train kit pays for itself again in Act 1 (Tilly's ore line).

DECISION NEEDED: What replaces the road and the tower in the slice?
Option A: **The Spillway (undercity) plus the Old Relay Tower recast as a Signals jammer factory around the ancient mast** — pros: both your examples (sewers and robot factory) in one stretch; same rooms, same climb, same three card fights and Kasp on the roof, so all approved map choices hold; the twist clues stay put / cons: train → slums → plant is close to FF7's opening hour (each piece borrowed once, plot ours; see Borrowed).
Option B: **Undercity only: go down, not up.** The mast's base is deep under the slums; descend floor by floor to Kasp in a jammer vault — pros: the least FF7 / cons: breaks the approved climb and the rooftop finale (Harrow's lights below), and going down fights the game's whole "always bigger, always up" shape.
Option C: **Keep the tower as it is; only the road becomes the undercity** — pros: the smallest change / cons: the ruined tower is still the classic tower dungeon, and there's no factory.
Recommendation: A. It's the biggest change you'll see for the smallest change to what's approved, and a rooftop boss over neon slums is a better end shot than a dusty ruin.

DECISION NEEDED: What are Act 2's two new places?
Option A: **Pit Nine (a company mining moon) and Breaker's Ring (a junk-ring city)**, built from kits already planned (slum, Works, Navy hull) — pros: two places that feel different from Harrow and each other, with no biome levels; about the same art as the approved "re-dressed moons"; both make the grim tone visible / cons: two more names to approve, and they share kit pieces you'll recognize up close.
Option B: **Three distinct places:** add a company planet (a corporate arcology city) — pros: the most variety; a real "next planet" before Slipway Prime / cons: one more town and dungeon built from scratch, the biggest single addition to your art list, and about 30 more minutes in your heaviest act.
Option C: **Keep the approved plan:** two Harrow-style moons with new palettes — pros: least work / cons: three dusty frontier towns in a row is the "same level, new color" problem your note is about.
Recommendation: A. It solves the trope problem at the same cost as the approved plan; per "practicality is taste", that's the one.
