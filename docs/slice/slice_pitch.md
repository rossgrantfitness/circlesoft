# Vertical Slice Pitch: Red, one town, one dungeon, one boss

> Owner: Creative Director (proposes) · Ross (decides). Date: 2026-10-09. Nothing here is final.
> You played the sandbox and said "its actuallly cool and totally bad ass" and "Proceed to the next phase of development". This is the plan for the next phase: the vertical slice. Six choices at the top, details below.

**The slice in one line:** Red, the hacker bunny with a sword, lives in a cute neon slum under a grim empire. She dives a dungeon, cuts through Signals cops with the Kingdom Hearts combat you just played, hacks things with the second button, and beats a boss whose weak spots you open with hacks. About 30 to 45 minutes to the first boss kill.

**How we square "cute towns and people" with the grim world:** the cute lives in the **people and what they make** (warm stalls, odd friendly neighbours, string lights, home cooking). The grim lives in the **system and the skyline** (checkpoints, propaganda screens, the Signals tower looming over everything) and in the dungeons. The town is the warm pocket you fight to protect. We are assuming the dungeons stay grim; tell us if not.

## The decisions

DECISION NEEDED: How does the game loop work?
Option A: **Mega Man Legends.** A hub town and hand-made dungeons, with the story moving you from one to the next. Finish a dungeon, do something in town, and the next one opens. — Pros: the shape you first described; every room is hand-tuned; best for story and characters; cheapest slice (about 4 to 5 weeks). / Cons: once you've played a dungeon it's done, so it doesn't replay; random-affix swords have little room (drops are fixed); every new hour of game means new hand-built rooms.
Option B: **Roguelike runs from a hub.** Each run is new floors built by the computer, random-affix swords drop, you die and start over stronger. Borrowing Binding of Isaac's surprise loot and Hades' hub. — Pros: endless replay; your affix swords become the heart of the game; more play hours per room built. / Cons: computer-built floors tend to look samey and are hard to make look good with hand-made art; the story and the cute town get squeezed; balancing random affixes is a lot of work; it can turn into grind, which you hate. The most expensive slice (about 6 to 8 weeks), because the floor builder, the affix system and the run economy all have to exist before it's fun.
Option C: **Hybrid, Hades-style.** A hand-made hub town; dungeon runs that change each time; the story moves forward between runs. The rooms are still hand-made (so they look like ours), but each run shuffles which rooms you get, the enemy mix and the sword drops. Dying sends you home, where people react and something new opens, so no run is wasted. — Pros: keeps the town and its people in every session; runs replay; affix swords fit naturally; dying moves the story on instead of feeling like grind; a "break the cycle" story fits a loop that repeats. / Cons: two systems to tune (story and runs); the writing has to handle repeat visits; the slice shows only a short taste of how runs grow. About 5 to 6 weeks.
Recommendation: **C, with hand-made rooms shuffled rather than computer-built floors.** It gives you all three things you've asked for (a living town, swords with random affixes, an action game worth replaying) at a middle cost, and it avoids both the "done once" problem of A and the grind risk of B.

DECISION NEEDED: Which town is the slice town?
Option A: **Harrow Landing's night market.** The slum town we already built, re-dressed as a cosy neon market: noodle carts under tarps, a bootleg arcade, a grandma running a charging stall out of her window, kids racing scrap drones, string lights and Ross's Japanese signage. Above it all, the Signals tower and its propaganda screens loom, and patrols pass through on the hour. Red's hideout (a back room over a repair shop) is home and the start of every run. — Pros: reuses the 9 built rooms, about 25 townsfolk, both shops and the job board; the clearest "cute people, grim skyline" picture. / Cons: the familiar choice; the old lamp-themed names (like Lamp Square) need new ones.
Option B: **The Stacks.** A vertical shanty town stacked under the ore line: laundry lines, rooftop gardens in oil drums, a tea stand on a fire escape. It has two moods: by day it's warm and busy; when the curfew siren goes, searchlights sweep and Red sneaks and hacks cameras to get around. — Pros: the town itself has gameplay; shows the cosy and the oppression side by side. / Cons: two dressings of every room; patrol and sneaking code in town; a vertical town is awkward for the fixed town camera. Most art for you.
Option C: **The Warren.** One cosy hacker co-op hidden in the hold of a beached ore freighter: bunks, a kitchen, a server rack, a workshop. It starts nearly empty. People Red rescues on runs move in and open services (a cook, a sword tinkerer, a shop), the Ys VIII village and Hades house idea. — Pros: the least to build at first; it grows as you play, which suits runs (loop B or C). / Cons: one building, not streets, so it's less "living town"; weaker on Mega Man Legends.
Recommendation: **A, with Red's hideout as the run start.** It's built, it's the strongest cute-in-grim picture, and Option C's "rescued people move in" idea can be borrowed into the market for free.

DECISION NEEDED: Which dungeon is the slice dungeon?
Option A: **The Jammer Works.** The Signals' factory built around Harrow's ancient radio mast, climbed floor by floor: cable mill, drone assembly line, freight lifts, the old mast at the bottom. Full of things to hack: turrets, doors, cranes, drone lines. Ends on the roof. — Pros: already designed (the approved tower map) and its puzzle pieces are built (card gates, power switches, cage lifts, push crates); the best place to show off hacking. / Cons: on foot only, unless we add a robot moment (see recommendation).
Option B: **The Scrap Hangar (the robot one).** Through the Spillway drains into a buried robot hangar. Halfway in, Red hacks a loader robot awake and smashes through the second half in it; at the end she docks the loader into the colossus and the boss fight is huge robot against huge robot. Built straight from the robot test you just played. — Pros: the biggest "totally bad ass" moment; the robot feel already exists. / Cons: the most expensive (a huge-scale boss and a huge-scale arena); it spends the whole on foot, small robot, huge robot climb in the first 40 minutes, so later acts have less to escalate to.
Option C: **The Dead Ore Line.** Abandoned rail tunnels under the slums and the derelict train cars rotting in them. Each car is a self-contained room, so they shuffle naturally. Ends at a derelict loading tower. — Pros: the best shape for runs; reuses the train kit planned for the opening. / Cons: tunnels and train cars can feel samey; less height and less to hack than the Works.
Recommendation: **A, with one bite of B.** Midway up the Works there's a freight bay where Red hacks a loader robot awake and smashes through one big room in it (the sandbox loader, as is). The colossus and the docking stay for a later act, so the scale climb still has somewhere to go. The Works' floors are built as hand-made rooms that shuffle, for loop C. If you want the docking in the slice, pick B.

DECISION NEEDED: Which boss ends the slice?
Option A: **Sergeant Kasp in the Hushmaster.** The petty Signals sergeant (the beaver who talks in memos) rides a spider-legged jammer walker. Readable patterns, Kingdom Hearts style: *Leg Stomp* (a leg glows and lifts, then slams a shock ring: jump it), *Dish Sweep* (a red line crawls across the floor before the beam follows it: dash through), *Drone Drop* (three drones: EMP clears them), *Quiet Hours* (the dish hums and the screen edges fizz, then Red's hacks are locked out for five seconds: hit the dish to cut it short). Hacking is the weakness: each leg has a relay box; zap it or turn a wall turret on it and the leg drops. Four legs down and the rig topples, and a prompt on the hack button lets Red jack into the dish for a big hit. Last phase: Kasp on foot with his clipboard and whistle. — Pros: an approved villain with personality; the legs make hacking matter; built on the approved boss design and keeps the story's groundwork. / Cons: needs Kasp and the walker modelled.
Option B: **The Precinct, a riot mech (robot fight).** A Signals riot-cop mech piloted by a wolf captain, fought with Red in the loader. *Shield Charge* (the shield glows and it stamps twice before charging), *Foam Cannon* (sticky puddles that slow you), *Siren Howl* (spinning siren lights, then a stun cone). Its firewall shield drops to an EMP; hacked shoulder turrets turn on it. Paired with dungeon B, the second phase moves to the colossus after docking. — Pros: robots and cops, both things you've made or asked for; huge spectacle. / Cons: the most costly boss, especially the huge phase; a new villain with no story yet.
Option C: **FOREMAN, the factory itself.** The Works' security system is the boss: a core behind a firewall cage, crane arms that swing after a horn and warning lights, a conveyor floor that shifts, turrets firing in set patterns. Red hacks the turrets onto its side and cracks the cage. Kasp yells at you from the monitors the whole time. — Pros: cheapest on your art (no new character model, mostly Works kit pieces); easy to remix for repeat runs. / Cons: no face to punch; less personality than a villain on screen.
Recommendation: **A.** A charismatic villain you can punch, with hacks as the key, on patterns players read at a glance. Kasp's memo voice is the cute-and-grim tone in one character.

DECISION NEEDED: Is this Red's starter set of hacks for the slice?
The second button (where the sandbox now says "Hack: coming later") fires the chosen hack; a small battery bar refills by landing sword hits, so hacking rewards staying aggressive. Mox builds the hardware; Red writes the hacks.
- **Zap Drone:** a little drone flies to the locked-on enemy and zaps it (ranged damage; the Kingdom Hearts Fire/Thunder job).
- **EMP:** a ring blast around Red that knocks enemies back, stuns drones and robots, and breaks guards (the crowd-control job).
- **Overclock:** hijack a nearby turret, drone or robot to fight for Red for ten seconds (the hacker's own trick).
- **Reboot:** restart Red's suit to heal; costs the whole battery (the Cure job).
Option A: **Approve these four.** — Pros: covers damage, crowd control, a signature hacker move and healing, the same jobs Kingdom Hearts magic does. / Cons: Overclock needs turrets and drones placed in every dungeon.
Option B: **Approve three, drop Overclock.** — Pros: cheaper; no level dressing needed. / Cons: loses the move that feels most like hacking.
Recommendation: **A.** Overclock is the one that makes Red a hacker rather than a wizard with a new coat of paint; the Works is full of turrets anyway.

DECISION NEEDED (optional, title can wait): Want a working title for now?
Option A: **No title yet.** Keep it "untitled". — Pros: no pressure; the title can come from the finished game. / Cons: files and builds keep saying "untitled".
Option B: **Pick one as a working title only.** Ideas (each needs a name and trademark check before it's used): **SIGNAL RED** (her name, and the signal she won't let them jam) · **ROOT ACCESS** (hacker slang; "root" as in home) · **NO SIGNAL** (what the Signals want; what every jammed screen says) · **EARS UP** (cute, and how Red looks when she's fired up) · **RED//BREACH**. — Pros: something to call the builds. / Cons: working titles tend to stick.
Recommendation: **A,** as you said, the title comes later. If one of these jumps out at you, we'll use it as a working name.

## What we reuse

**From the combat sandbox (all of it):** Red's controller, the one-button Kingdom Hearts combo, dash, jump, guard, lock-on and camera; the Cyberwolf and the Brute with their AI (dodge, block, flank, flee, enrage); hit-stop, shake, trails and sparks; your six swords and the sword swap; the loader robot, the colossus, docking and the scale profiles; the HUD, pause menu and feel-knobs panel; about 2,400 automated checks.

**From the shelved turn-based game (saved at commit 9b61011):**
- **Harrow Landing graybox:** 9 rooms, about 25 townsfolk with lines, both shops, the job board, two side deliveries, hidden items. Becomes the night market.
- **The tower map and the Works puzzle pieces:** card gates, power switches, cage lifts, push crates, bells. Become the Jammer Works.
- **Save system, menus, inventory and shops, dialogue with speech bubbles and gibberish voices, scene changes.** All carry over almost as is.
- **Kasp's boss design** (legs knocked out one by one, the jam pulse). The design carries over; the turn-based code doesn't.
- **The battle camera director** (your storyboard) becomes the boss intro.

**The cast and world, in new jobs:**
- **Red** goes from scrap courier to data runner: same job, cyberpunk version.
- **Mox** is the workshop (he builds her gadgets, the Mega Man Legends Roll role).
- **Otis** runs the forge and the sword tinkering.
- **Vela** is the voice in Red's ear on runs (a Signals listener who switched sides).
- **Watch Zero's old-timers** are the town's loudest neighbours.
- **The Signals Corps** is the enemy: cops, checkpoints, memos.

**Your art:** Red, the Cyberwolf, six swords, both city texture packs (clean and busted).

**Not asked here, coming separately:** the story. The slice can run on the approved Harrow setup (the Signals jamming the old mast, Kasp, Watch Zero) with the lamp names swapped out, so it doesn't wait on a story rewrite. Nothing gets renamed or removed from the build until you say so.

## Size and cost

**Studio time** (code, levels, tuning, tests, two or three feel rounds with you):

| Loop | Slice time | Main cost driver |
|---|---|---|
| A, Mega Man Legends | about 4 to 5 weeks | hand-building every room |
| **C, Hades-style (recommended)** | **about 5 to 6 weeks** | A, plus room shuffling, the run reset and between-run unlocks |
| B, Roguelike | about 6 to 8 weeks | the floor builder, the affix system, run balance |

Picking dungeon B or boss B (the full robot finale) adds about 1 to 2 weeks to any of these. The Level Designer you approved for this point joins at the start. Your art sets the calendar more than our code does: we build on placeholders and swap your models in as they arrive.

**What you'd make (recommended path), in rough priority order:**
1. **Kasp** (beaver, Signals uniform) and **the Hushmaster** (spider-legged jammer walker).
2. **One or two more enemy types** to sit beside your Cyberwolf (a Signals drone; a heavy to replace the Brute blockout).
3. **Town people:** Otis, Mox, Vela, plus 3 or 4 market residents (the rest are colour swaps).
4. **Town kit:** a handful of market stalls, carts and signs (your city textures cover the walls and floors).
5. **Works kit:** a few machines, a turret, a terminal, the freight bay.
6. **Small items:** the Zap Drone model, four hack icons, dialogue portraits for the 4 or 5 main speakers.

The full list goes into docs/art_requests.md (sizes, formats, where each goes) once you've picked.

**What stays placeholder:** all animations (the free Quaternius set), the two robots, VFX, sounds and music, generic townsfolk (colour swaps), and anything not on the list above.
