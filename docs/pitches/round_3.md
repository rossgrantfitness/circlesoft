# Pitch Round 3

> Status: **IN PROGRESS** · Creative Director drafting · Technical Director and Producer review next · then to Ross for sign-off.

## Ross's brief (2026-10-05)

### THE MAIN IDEA
**The heroes aren't the first.** Midway through the game we discover that others have tried to do exactly what our heroes are doing, and that these events have played out over and over, endlessly, throughout history. Our heroes are the ones who finally **break the cycle**. In feel, this is like the reveals in The Matrix Reloaded and Gurren Lagann.

### The shape of the game
- **A classic JRPG hero's journey.** A clear hero, a call to adventure, a party gathered along the way, a world to save.
- **An unexpected twist in the middle:** the cycle reveal above.
- **A huge climax.**
- **The climb in scale:** on foot → big robot → giant robot → giant spaceship → intergalactic god.

### Theme
- **Death and rebirth are themes, not gameplay systems.** No dying-as-a-mechanic, no rebirth rules, no retry gimmicks.
- **The theme is unspoken.** Never stated outright, never lectured, never put in a character's mouth. It's understated and left for players to interpret.

### Characters and world (carried over)
- **Characters:** anime anthropomorphic chibi animal characters, every one of them. The story never makes a big deal of them being animals. They carry the same gravity as people; the writing "forgets" they're animals, the art doesn't.
- **Party-based:** a main character with a real supporting cast.
- **Setting:** sci-fi first, with fantasy and animal anime elements.
- **Feel:** long cutscenes; a look like Mega Man Legends or Fear Effect; loves FF7, MGS1, Fallout 3, Chrono Trigger/Cross, FF8, FF9, FFX, DQ7.

### What didn't work before
- Round 1: animal gimmicks; not enough robots or spaceships.
- Round 2: high-concept premises (the hero dies and becomes the robot; court battles; a growing seed) instead of a classic hero's journey, and the theme was built into the mechanics and said out loud.

## Studio guardrails
- **Original.** The "it's all happened before" twist is a broad, classic idea and is welcome. What we must not copy is any particular work's version of it: no Architect-style room of screens or "you are the sixth one"; no previous hero who gave up and became the tyrant to stop the cycle; no pilgrimage that ends in a ritual sacrifice to buy a few years of peace; no drill or spiral motif. No names, characters, monsters, music or story beats from existing games.
- The vertical slice (one town, one dungeon, one boss, three party members, full battle system, ~30 min of story) comes from each pitch's opening, so it starts on foot, and it should quietly plant the first clue of the cycle.
