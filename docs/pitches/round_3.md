# Pitch Round 3

> Status: **DECIDED 2026-10-05** · Ross chose **Pitch A: LIGHTS LEFT ON**.

## Ross's brief (2026-10-05)

### THE MAIN IDEA
**The heroes aren't the first.** Midway through the game we discover that others have tried to do exactly what our heroes are doing, and that these events have played out over and over, endlessly, throughout history. Our heroes are the ones who finally **break the cycle**. In feel, this is like the reveals in The Matrix Reloaded and Gurren Lagann.

### The shape of the game
- **A classic JRPG hero's journey.** A clear hero, a call to adventure, a party gathered along the way, a world to save.
- **An unexpected twist in the middle:** the cycle reveal above.
- **A huge climax.**
- **The climb in scale:** on foot → big robot → giant robot → giant spaceship → intergalactic god.
- **Space!** The game takes place in space, at least partly: planets, moons, stations, starships, travel between worlds. Not a single planet that only reaches space at the very end.

### Theme
- **Death and rebirth are themes, not gameplay systems.** No dying-as-a-mechanic, no rebirth rules, no retry gimmicks.
- **The theme is unspoken.** Never stated outright, never lectured, never put in a character's mouth. It's understated and left for players to interpret.

### Characters and world (carried over)
- **Characters:** anime anthropomorphic chibi animal characters, every one of them. The story never makes a big deal of them being animals. They carry the same gravity as people; the writing "forgets" they're animals, the art doesn't.
- **Party-based:** a main character with a real supporting cast.
- **Setting:** sci-fi first, with fantasy and animal anime elements.
- **Feel:** long cutscenes; a look like Mega Man Legends or Fear Effect; loves FF7, MGS1, Fallout 3, Chrono Trigger/Cross, FF8, FF9, FFX, DQ7.

### What didn't work before
- Round 1: animal gimmicks; not enough robots or spaceships.
- Round 2: high-concept premises (the hero dies and becomes the robot; court battles; a growing seed) instead of a classic hero's journey, and the theme was built into the mechanics and said out loud.

## Studio guardrails
- **Original.** The "it's all happened before" twist is a broad, classic idea and is welcome. What we must not copy is any particular work's version of it: no Architect-style room of screens or "you are the sixth one"; no previous hero who gave up and became the tyrant to stop the cycle; no pilgrimage that ends in a ritual sacrifice to buy a few years of peace; no drill or spiral motif. No names, characters, monsters, music or story beats from existing games.
- The vertical slice (one town, one dungeon, one boss, three party members, full battle system, ~30 min of story) comes from each pitch's opening, so it starts on foot, and it should quietly plant the first clue of the cycle.

## The pitches

### Pitch A: LIGHTS LEFT ON (alt: THE QUIET BETWEEN)
**Logline:** For ten years, a young courier's missing mother has been sending a distress call from the edge of known space. The courier answers it, and has to fight an empire to get there.

**The world:** The Outer Marches are a frontier of dust moons, ring stations and trading ports strung along the edge of the galaxy, ruled by the Hegemony from its shipyard capital. Ships hop between worlds on old jump lanes. Past the last lane is the Quiet, a starless dark where ships disappear, and a faint distress call has come out of it for as long as anyone remembers. The Hegemony has made answering it a crime, and on every frontier moon families keep a lamp in the window for someone who went out there.

**The hero:** Pell Arden. A chibi fennec fox with enormous ears, her mother's flight jacket rolled at the sleeves, goggles, and a hand lamp clipped to her belt. A scrap courier on the dust moon Harrow who still lights her mother's window lamp every night. Stubborn hope and a sharp tongue: she'd rather be wrong and moving than right and waiting. *"Somebody called for help. I don't need a better reason than that."*

**The party (3–4):**
- **Osric Dunmore:** chibi walrus, brass-capped tusks, dockworker's coveralls. Tank and pilot: a gentle ex-Hegemony mech pilot who hums old work songs and never raises his voice. Joins in the slice as Harrow's dock boss; at the end of Act 1 Pell "borrows" his cargo walker.
- **Mox:** chibi ferret kid with a welding mask and too many pockets. Mechanic and gadgets; brags constantly, panics right on schedule. Joins in the slice by stowing away in Pell's delivery crate.
- **Wren Tallow:** chibi heron in a pressed signals uniform, earpiece always in. Healer and support: prim, exact, speaks seven languages and none of them casually. Ordered to jam the call, she listens to it instead and defects at the end of Act 1.
- **Ruo Vantell:** chibi raccoon smuggler captain in a long coat, with a worse reputation. Runs blockades and flirts with danger; he lost his brother to the call years ago. Joins in Act 2 when the party needs a ship to the Rim.

**The villain:** Admiral Sarrow Vane, a chibi stoat in a white winter greatcoat. He built the Hegemony's navy from machines dragged out of the edge of the Quiet, and he wants whatever lies at its heart for his fleet.

**The journey:**
- **Act 1:** Hegemony troops come to Harrow to silence the call. Pell fights them on foot, then takes Osric's cargo walker (big robot) to break out and get off-world.
- **Act 2:** Hopping moons and stations across the Marches, the party steals the Hegemony's prize titan (giant robot), "salvaged from somewhere nobody will name," to smash the blockade at the Rim and cross into the Quiet. The twist is waiting there.
- **Act 3:** They fly her mother's recovered ship (giant spaceship) into the deep Quiet with Vane's fleet behind them, down to the Keeper, a god as wide as the gap between galaxies.

**The midpoint twist (the cycle):** Inside the Quiet, the party finds Pell's mother's ship drifting empty, and its log shows she was answering a call from the crew before hers. Around it floats a harbor of empty ships from every century, each one brought by a hero and a party who heard a call, fought their way out here, and were taken by the Keeper, which holds on to everything that reaches it. Each lost crew's last call for help became the next crew's call to adventure, and the cycle has run for a thousand years on people refusing to abandon each other.

**Breaking the cycle (the climax):** At the moment when every crew before them sent out a call for help, Pell switches the beacon off so no one else will ever have to follow, and the party goes deeper instead. They wake every crew the Keeper has held, and a fleet of ships from a thousand years of history turns on the Keeper together, breaks its hold, and flies home.

**The unspoken theme:** Lamps in windows; a starless silence that swallows sound; crews found asleep and unaged in amber light; the last shot is lamps going out in windows all across the Marches, because nobody needs them anymore.

**Battle system hook:** Classic turn-based party battles with Relay: any character can hand their turn to the next, and every hand-off in a row adds power to whoever finally acts, but the chain drops if anyone gets hit before the last one goes. The same Relay runs through the crew's stations in walker, titan and ship battles, so the system scales up without changing.

**Vertical slice:** Town is Harrow Landing, a dust-moon port of stacked cargo containers with a lamp in every window. Dungeon is the Old Relay Tower, a half-buried signal mast where the call is loudest and a Hegemony crew is installing a jammer; boss is Sergeant Kasp in a spider-legged jammer rig. Party is Pell, Osric and Mox, all on foot. First clue: the beacon part Pell recovers plays her mother's voice, and underneath it, barely audible, a second voice says the same words in an old accent.

**Stays original:** No machines running a reset, no numbered chosen one, no hero turned tyrant, no sacrifice that buys a few years of peace, no drills or spirals. Here the cycle is rescuers lost one after another, and it's broken by the first crew to come home.

**🔧 Technical Director: Medium.** The slice is straightforward, and Relay is just a turn-passing rule that works the same in every vehicle. The trickiest piece is the climax fleet of hundreds of ships. To lower the risk, show the fleet in a cutscene reusing a few ship models, and have the player fight only a handful.

**🎨 Producer (slice art): Medium.** 3 hero chibis (Pell, Osric, Mox) plus 5–6 townsfolk; 4 portraits (including Sgt. Kasp); 2–3 Hegemony trooper and drone enemies; a boss on a spider-legged walker (the legs are tricky to animate); 2 sets: Harrow Landing and the Old Relay Tower. Unusual: night lighting with a glowing lamp in every window.

### Pitch B: THE LAST CROWN (alt: EVER AFTER)
**Logline:** A noodle-delivery kid on a backwater space station rises to overthrow the Gilded Prince, the tyrant who rules a whole cluster of worlds from his golden fleet.

**The world:** The Hundred Lights are a cluster of worlds, from tea-farming moons to ringed ocean planets, busy with freighters, ferries and pirate skiffs. For twenty years they've been ruled by the Gilded Prince, a nobody until a "star-crown" fell from the sky into his hands and gave him an army of machines. Every school teaches legends of heroes who toppled tyrants long ago, and every station has a mural of them. Tone: big-hearted, funny and rousing, then a gut punch.

**The hero:** Tobin Hart. A chibi corgi with huge ears, a long scarf, boots two sizes too big and a dented delivery thermos. He runs noodles across the docks of Port Sundry, a station-town bolted to an asteroid, and knows every hero mural by heart. A loud, sincere optimist who thinks the old stories are instructions. *"Every story starts with somebody too dumb to stay home. I'm qualified."*

**The party (3–4):**
- **Ottoline Prewitt:** chibi hippo, retired champion wrestler and Port Sundry's dock boss, in a cardigan. Tank: a soft heart with a terrifying body slam. Joins in the slice when the Prince's tithe-men take the station's water.
- **Mags Delacroix:** chibi skunk in grease-stained coveralls and welding goggles. Engineer and robot pilot; dry, impatient, built the Prince's war walkers and hates what they're used for. Joins in the slice, hiding in the tithe depot with her stolen prototype plans.
- **Camille Moraine:** chibi hyena in a court gown cut short for running. Mage: the Gilded Prince's younger sister, elegant, guilty and braver than she looks. Runs away from her brother's court to join in Act 1.

**The villain:** The Gilded Prince, Caspar Moraine, a chibi hyena in golden armor who narrates his own victories on every broadcast channel. Cruel and theatrical, funny until he isn't.

**The journey:**
- **Act 1:** Tobin's crew fights the Prince's tithe-men on foot. Mags turns her prototype into Mule, a walker (big robot), and the rebellion spreads moon to moon.
- **Act 2:** A kindly old lighthouse keeper hands them Old Brass, a titan (giant robot) he's kept hidden for fifty years, and won't say why. They take the war to the Prince's capital and beat him in a titan duel. Then the credits roll. That's the midpoint.
- **Act 3:** The party refits the Prince's flagship (giant spaceship) and crosses the dark to the next galaxy to face the Far Throne (intergalactic god).

**The midpoint twist (the cycle):** After the fake credits, Old Okke, the pelican who runs the noodle stall Tobin delivers for, takes a medal out of a drawer and says, "Give it ten years." Every tyrant in history was crowned by the same god, the Far Throne, which rules the next galaxy over and drops a new star-crown on someone desperate whenever the last tyrant falls. Every hero party in history beat their tyrant, enjoyed the parade and went home, including the kind old strangers Tobin has met all game (Okke, the lighthouse keeper, his old teacher), because nobody ever followed the crown back to where it came from.

**Breaking the cycle (the climax):** When the next crown falls, the party follows its trail back across the dark between galaxies, in a ship refitted by every retired hero in the Hundred Lights, and becomes the first heroes ever to go past their own happy ending. They fight the Far Throne across its own galaxy of crowned worlds and bring it down, and for the first time in history no crown falls anywhere.

**The unspoken theme:** The fake ending is a small death and Act 3 is the waking after it; the old heroes quietly age as the game goes on; the last scene is the noodle stall, with Okke's stool empty and Tobin serving a kid who has stopped looking up at the sky.

**Battle system hook:** Classic turn-based party battles with Weak Points: every enemy, from a tithe-man to the god, has a body map of parts, and breaking a part takes away what it can do (break the gun arm and it can't shoot). Scale changes what a "part" is: limbs on a soldier, decks on a flagship, whole worlds on the god.

**Vertical slice:** Town is Port Sundry, a ramshackle asteroid station of noodle stalls, ferry docks and faded hero murals. Dungeon is the Tithe Depot, where the Prince's men are hoarding the station's water; boss is Tithe-Captain Ondra Skell, a chibi crocodile in a collector walker. Party is Tobin, Ottoline and Mags, all on foot. First clue: a faded photo on Okke's stall wall shows five young people in battle gear in front of a fallen golden wreck, and he says it was "a costume party."

**Stays original:** No machines running a reset, no numbered chosen one, no hero turned tyrant (each tyrant is a new, desperate person, and the old heroes simply went home), no sacrifice for a few years of peace, no drills or spirals. Here the cycle is winning without finishing the job, and the fake ending is our own trick.

**🔧 Technical Director: Hard.** The slice is fine, but every enemy needs its own map of breakable parts, art for each broken part, and new tactics as it loses pieces. The trickiest piece is that art and tuning load. To lower the risk, give full part maps to bosses only and one or two parts to ordinary enemies.

**🎨 Producer (slice art): Heavy for a slice.** 3 hero chibis (Tobin, Ottoline, Mags) plus Okke and townsfolk; 5 portraits (including Okke and Skell); 2–3 tithe-man enemies; a boss in a collector walker; 2 sets: Port Sundry and the Tithe Depot. Unusual: every enemy needs breakable parts with damaged versions, plus several painted hero murals and an old photo.

### Pitch C: DIMSTAR (alt: SUNCHASERS)
**Logline:** When the sun over a frozen mining moon starts going dark, a young lineman sets out across the star system to find out why, and runs straight into a ruler who wants to turn the cause into a weapon.

**The world:** The Calder system is a ring of colony moons, orbital greenhouses and freight stations around a golden sun, with jump routes out to neighboring stars. For three years Calder has been dimming: the outer moons are freezing, harvests are failing, and the inner worlds are hoarding fuel. Old star charts show dead and dim stars scattered across the galaxy that nobody can explain. Tone: frost and firelight, frontier grit, a slow mystery that turns into a chase across the stars.

**The hero:** Lio Farrant. A chibi lynx with tufted ears, a patched thermal poncho and a lineman's hook-staff. He keeps the heat lines running in the mining town of Lampwick on the moon Gloam. Duty-bound, dry-humored, and physically unable to sit still while anyone is cold. *"If the sun won't come to us, we'll go knock on its door."*

**The party (3–4):**
- **Peb Ollery:** chibi capybara in a straw sunhat and several cardigans, Lampwick's greenhouse keeper and Lio's foster aunt. Healer: unshakably calm, never in a hurry, nearly always right. Joins in the slice to keep Lio alive.
- **Professor Umberto Quill:** chibi hedgehog with a cracked monocle and a telescope on his back. Magic user who fights with star charts; a disgraced astronomer with a theory about the dead stars that everyone laughs at. Joins in the slice when soldiers wreck his observatory.
- **Captain Vesna Kroll:** chibi moose in a heavy officer's coat, antlers wrapped in signal ribbons. Tank and walker pilot: by the book and loyal, until her ruler orders the outer moons left to freeze. Defects at the end of Act 1.

**The villain:** Margrave Isolde Dray, a chibi black swan who rules the inner worlds from a palace station. She's the first to understand what's inside the sun, and she plans to wake it and steer it onto her rivals.

**The journey:**
- **Act 1:** The Margrave's troops strip Gloam's fuel. Lio and friends fight them on foot, then seize an ice-cutter walker (big robot) and break through her blockade into space.
- **Act 2:** Moon to moon and station to station, Quill's theory takes shape. They capture the Margrave's siege titan (giant robot), smash her cordon around the sun and dive into Calder's fire. The twist is waiting inside.
- **Act 3:** The Margrave wakes what's in the sun and herds it toward her enemies with her fleet. The heroes seize her flagship (giant spaceship) and chase the god (intergalactic god) across the stars.

**The midpoint twist (the cycle):** The sun is dimming because a god from between the galaxies has lain down inside it to rest, the way it has lain down in star after star for ten thousand years; the dead stars on Quill's charts are its old beds. Every world it touched raised heroes like ours, who woke it and drove it off to someone else's sky, and each of those worlds still has a statue of its heroes holding up a sun, which the party has walked past all game thinking it was a common religion. It was never attacking anyone: every hero in history won, and every win just moved it next door.

**Breaking the cycle (the climax):** The party won't drive it on. After a battle across the whole system against a god half-mad from ten thousand years of being chased, they turn the flagship and lead it out to a dead star where no one lives, hold off the Margrave's fleet while it settles, and leave it sleeping, left alone for the first time in history.

**The unspoken theme:** Cold and warmth; a sun that fades and comes back; a god that only wants to lie down; the last shot, years later, is a faint new glow in the dead star where it sleeps, and the first green in Peb's greenhouse.

**Battle system hook:** Classic turn-based party battles with Range Rings: both sides stand on near, middle and far rings, and moving between them changes which moves work (Lio's hook pulls enemies in, Quill wants distance). The rings stretch with scale: arm's length on foot, city blocks for the titan, thousands of miles for the fleet.

**Vertical slice:** Town is Lampwick, a mining town on Gloam strung with heater cables, its market huddled around one huge furnace under a weak brown sun. Dungeon is the Furnace Works, the town's heat plant, which the Margrave's soldiers are stripping; boss is Lieutenant Graw, a chibi wolverine in a pumping rig rebuilt as a walker. Party is Lio, Peb and Quill, all on foot. First clue: Quill's chart shows a line of dead stars curving across the sky toward Calder. He calls it a coincidence, and nobody argues.

**Stays original:** No machines running a reset, no numbered chosen one, no hero turned tyrant, no sacrifice, and no monster that keeps coming back to the same place. Here every hero in history won, the god moves on instead of returning, and the cycle breaks when someone finally lets it rest. No drills or spirals.

**🔧 Technical Director: Medium.** Tracking near, middle and far is simple. The trickiest piece is making enemies choose sensibly when to close in or back off, at every scale. To lower the risk, give each enemy a "favorite ring" in its data file and test the battle screen early with colored boxes.

**🎨 Producer (slice art): Medium.** 3 hero chibis (Lio, Peb, Quill) plus townsfolk; 4 portraits (including Lt. Graw); 2–3 soldier enemies; a boss in a pumping-rig walker; 2 sets: Lampwick and the Furnace Works. Unusual: frost and furnace-glow lighting under a dim brown sun, a "hero holding up a sun" statue, and a star-chart prop.

### Creative Director's recommendation
LIGHTS LEFT ON. It's the most classic hero's journey of the three, the twist hits the hero personally (the call she followed is the cycle itself), and a thousand years of lost crews flying home together is the biggest, most earned climax. THE LAST CROWN is the bold alternative: a fake ending at the midpoint is the kind of stunt players remember for decades.
