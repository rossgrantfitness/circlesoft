# Pitch Round 2

> Status: **IN PROGRESS** · Creative Director done · Technical Director and Producer reviewing · then to Ross for sign-off.

## Ross's brief (2026-10-05)

What Ross wants, in his words and ours:

- **The climb:** on foot → big robot → giant robot → giant spaceship → intergalactic god. Every stage of scale is a step up the ladder, all the way to fighting (and becoming) a god.
- **Setting:** sci-fi first, with fantasy and animal anime elements mixed in.
- **Characters:** anime anthropomorphic chibi animal characters, every one of them. But the story never makes a big deal of them being animals: no animal gimmicks, no 'nine lives'-style mechanics. They carry the same gravity as people; the writing 'forgets' they're animals, the art doesn't.
- **Party-based RPG:** a main character with a real supporting cast who travel and fight together.
- **Still true from round 1:** death, rebirth and "try, die, try again" as a theme; long cutscenes; a look like Mega Man Legends or Fear Effect; loves FF7, MGS1, Fallout 3, Chrono Trigger/Cross, FF8, FF9, FFX, DQ7.
- **What didn't work in round 1:** characters that were simply animals, and mechanics built on animal gimmicks. Not enough robots and spaceships.

## Studio guardrails
- Original: the escalating-scale climb is welcome, but no copying Gurren Lagann's signature beats (no drill motif, no spiral-energy equivalent, no copied opening or story beats), and nothing lifted from any other work.
- The vertical slice (one town, one dungeon, one boss, three party members, full battle system, ~30 min story) must come from each pitch's opening, so it starts at the "on foot" stage, with a taste of the robot to come.

## The pitches

All three: every character is a chibi anthro animal (big head, small body, expressive anime eyes, clothes and gear like any JRPG hero). The art shows the animal; the story never mentions it.

### Pitch A: IRONWOOD (alt: GROW A GOD)
**Logline:** A failed shrine-gardener's village heirloom, a metal seed that has never sprouted, finally starts to grow. She has to raise it from sapling to god before the people who planted it come back to harvest it.
**The world:** Greenreach is a farming colony of glass domes and terraced orchards at the edge of a trade empire. Every village shrine keeps a "kami-seed," a boulder-sized metal seed left by unknown makers. They never sprout, but their warmth powers the towns. Shrine-gardeners sing to them, corporations buy them up, raiders steal them. Tone: warm, wonder-filled, coming of age, with seasons passing act by act.
**The climb:**
- **On foot:** Juni and friends defend the orchard village with farm tools, shrine charms and a scrap rifle.
- **Big robot:** the seed sprouts into Sprig, a three-story walking engine plated in metal bark.
- **Giant robot:** Sprig roots down through a winter and wakes as Elder, mountain-sized, a walking fortress for the colony's war.
- **Giant spaceship:** Elder's crown hardens and lifts off as the Canopy, a living ship the party calls home.
- **Intergalactic god:** the Gardener, a god spread across galaxies that planted every seed. Juni fights it, Sprig blooms into a god, and Juni becomes its heart.
**Main character:** Juni Vass. A chibi red panda: russet fur, a big ringed tail, oversized gardening gloves, a straw hat with a cracked visor. Ex-apprentice gardener, kicked out of the shrine for "talking back to the seed." She's stubborn and tender, and treats machines like houseplants that need encouragement. *"It's not a weapon. It's a seedling. ...A seedling that just stepped on your truck."*
**Supporting cast:**
- **Hesper Vass:** chibi mountain goat with curled horns and shrine robes. Juni's aunt and head gardener, stern, swings a sledgehammer, and secretly prays the seed never wakes.
- **Dez Kahale:** chibi sea otter in a flight jacket. The raider who steals the seed, then switches sides when his boss turns on him. Charming liar, terrible at lying to Juni.
- **Inspector Ilse Varro:** chibi barn owl in a pressed corporate uniform. Sent to confiscate the seed, quietly sabotaging her own company.
**Battle hook:** Two-layer battles. The robot fights the big enemy on its own turn track while the party fights small enemies crawling over its body, and what the party does (clearing boarders, repairing, charging) powers the robot's big moves. You shape the robot by grafting parts from beaten enemies onto it, growing new skill branches. When it's destroyed, it drops a seed and regrows bigger and stronger next fight. As scale climbs, the party's layer goes from its shoulders, to its decks, to whole districts inside the ship.
**Twist (kind):** The seeds weren't gifts. Someone planted them for a reason, and Juni's isn't the first one to grow.
**Vertical slice:** Town is Saltgrass Terrace, a terraced orchard village under a cracked dome during harvest festival. Dungeon is the Thresher, a crashed harvester walker in the orchards where raiders have taken the seed. Boss is Foreman Gall, a raider in an armored loader. Mid-fight the seed sprouts and Sprig stands up for the final blow, the first taste of the robot.
**Stays original:** Growth comes from tending, grafting and seasons, not willpower energy. Roots, not drills. The seed is a shrine heirloom, not a buried find. No combining, and no mentor death.

### Pitch B: FIVE FUNERALS (alt: WAKE UP BIGGER)
**Logline:** On a mining moon where the dead keep working inside machines, a courier dies and wakes up as a robot. Her mind won't fade like everyone else's. It keeps growing, and every body she's given gets too small.
**The world:** On Low Haven, nobody retires, not even in death. When you die, your mind is caught in an "echo-core" and installed in a machine: grandma runs the crane, your old teacher is a tram. After a few years, minds fade and are released at a second, final funeral. Tone: bittersweet melodrama. Every act opens with Seri's own funeral, and every time she grows she loses something small: holding a hand, hearing a whisper.
**The climb:**
- **On foot:** Seri, alive, running deliveries through the mining town.
- **Big robot:** after her first funeral she wakes as a loader-frame, still herself.
- **Giant robot:** second funeral. Her mind outgrows the loader and she's moved into a decommissioned siege titan.
- **Giant spaceship:** third funeral. She becomes a warship, with her friends as her crew.
- **Intergalactic god:** the Last Mourner, a god that holds every dead mind in the galaxy and was once someone who kept growing too. The fifth funeral is either its or hers.
**Main character:** Seri Lowe. A chibi fawn: dappled coat, tiny antler nubs, a courier's satchel twice her size, a scarf she can't wear after she dies. Upbeat, reckless, and determined to make everyone else's grief easier than her own. *"Bury me light. I keep coming back heavier."*
**Supporting cast:**
- **Dr. Absalom Tuck:** chibi badger in a stained mortician's coat. The mortician-engineer who performs every transfer. Drinks, sings at funerals, and blames himself for each one.
- **Kiri Osei:** chibi hare with ears tied back under racing goggles. Seri's best friend, a pilot who refuses to let her go and climbs into every new body to fly it.
- **Warden Maro:** chibi tortoise in heavy grave-warden armor. Sent to shut Seri down because growing minds are forbidden. Slow, ancient-souled, and joins once she saves his life.
**Battle hook:** Seri is the party member who grows, and the party moves into her. On foot it's a normal party of three. As a big robot she's a giant ally fighting beside them. As a giant robot the others take stations in her limbs and their menus become her moves. As a ship, enemies board her and you defend her rooms while she fires broadsides. Losing a fight replays the moment she "almost died again," and she remembers it.
**Twist (kind):** Every body Seri wakes up in was built before she died, and someone is already building the next one.
**Vertical slice:** Town is Low Haven Shaft 4, a mining town of dead-run machines and lantern shrines. Dungeon is the Echo Kiln, the transfer plant, overrun after a core breach. Boss is the Unclaimed, a scrap giant made of minds nobody came to collect. Seri dies in the fight, and the slice ends with her first funeral, then she wakes as the loader-frame and finishes the boss in one big robot turn.
**Stays original:** The robot isn't found or piloted, she becomes it. The one who dies is the hero, on screen, every act, not a mentor. No drills, no energy motif, no combining.

### Pitch C: THE LONG APPEAL (alt: ORDEAL OF STARS)
**Logline:** A condemned thief is executed in the opening scene and revived for her appeal. She fights her case up through every court in the galaxy, each with bigger champions, until she stands before the god who judges them all.
**The world:** The Concord is a union of a thousand worlds where every dispute, from a stolen goat to a planetary war, is settled by Ordeal: champions fight, and the court rules for the winner. The bigger the court, the bigger the champion: duelists in villages, robots in districts, titans in high courts, warships at the Admiralty. The condemned are revived for every appeal, which makes death row very long. Tone: rain-soaked noir, stylish and dry, with strange humor (vendors sell appeal forms like noodles).
**The climb:**
- **On foot:** the village Ordeal, Calla and her lawyer against the magistrate's bailiffs, hand to hand.
- **Big robot:** District Court, where she's assigned a battered public-defender loaner robot nicknamed Exhibit B.
- **Giant robot:** High Court, city-sized champion duels that wreck the courthouse district.
- **Giant spaceship:** Admiralty Court, verdict by fleet battle, aboard a condemned prison ship she has to make fly.
- **Intergalactic god:** the Final Court, where the judge is a god of law that spans galaxies. Win the last appeal and you inherit the gavel.
**Main character:** Calla Rook. A chibi magpie: glossy black-and-white feathers, a long prison coat, cuffs she wears like bracelets, a stolen silver ring. A thief with a sharp tongue who refuses to say what she actually did. *"Sentence me again. I'll appeal again. You keep giving me more time."*
**Supporting cast:**
- **Counselor Augustin Fell:** chibi basset hound in a rumpled suit. A washed-up public defender who has never won a case. Drinks, mumbles, and is secretly brilliant.
- **Sister Ondine Hask:** chibi hammerhead shark in an executioner's habit. The law makes her escort the condemned through every appeal. Polite, deadpan, kind, and will execute Calla if she loses.
- **Fennel Brask:** chibi bison in a mechanic's apron. The one juror who voted not guilty, who quits jury duty to keep their loaner robot running.
**Battle hook:** Every Ordeal has a Statute, a court rule for that fight ("the accused may not heal," "one blow per turn"). You can spend a turn filing a Motion to change it, using a party member's skill as the argument. As scale climbs, so do the laws: district robot dueling codes, then city-wrecking titan rules, then fleet law. Game over is an execution cutscene, and Continue is "File Appeal."
**Twist (kind):** Calla is guilty, just not of the crime on the charge sheet, and the god on the bench knows exactly what she did.
**Vertical slice:** Town is Penance Row, a rainy court district where every street corner is a tribunal. Dungeon is the Evidence Vault, broken into the night before the appeal to steal back proof. Boss is the magistrate's champion, Bailiff Corvane, in the first Ordeal. Calla wins on foot, and the slice ends with her first District hearing in Exhibit B.
**Stays original:** The robots are court-assigned champions, not dug up or stolen. The escalation is legal appeals. No drills, no energy motif, no combining, no mentor death.

### Creative Director's recommendation
FIVE FUNERALS. It builds Ross's death-and-rebirth theme into the climb itself, and a slice that ends with the hero's funeral and her waking up as a robot is a hook players won't forget. It's also the hardest to build, so if the Technical Director flags the body-switching as too risky, THE LONG APPEAL is the best fallback for style and tone.
