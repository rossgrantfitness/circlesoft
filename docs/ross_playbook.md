# Ross's Playbook

> **What this is:** the studio's memory of Ross's taste: every decision and piece of feedback he gives, distilled into principles the studio can follow. It's the guiding force for creative work, and the evidence Ross uses to decide when the studio can work on its own.
> **Keeper:** the Taste Keeper agent (logs, distills, predicts). **Authority:** Ross. Nothing here overrides a fresh instruction from him.
> **Every agent:** read the Principles before any creative or design work. If the Playbook doesn't cover something, ask Ross; don't guess.

## 2. Principles

Distilled from the Feedback Log. Each cites its source entries (F#). Newest evidence wins when they conflict.

### Story & structure
- **Classic JRPG hero's journey over high-concept premises.** A hero, a call to adventure, a party gathered on the road, a world to save. The high-concept round 2 pitches were all rejected for this. (F6, F7)
- **The signature twist: the heroes aren't the first.** It's all happened before, over and over, and our heroes are the ones who break the cycle. He asked for it "in the middle of the game, followed by a huge climax" (F7), but then approved an outline with it about 7 of 10 hours in (F19, newer), so the exact timing is not a hard rule. (F7, F19)
- **Escalating scale is non-negotiable:** on foot → big robot → giant robot → giant spaceship → intergalactic god. More robots and spaceships, not fewer: round 1 was rejected partly for not delivering this. (F2, F3; carried into F7 per decisions.md)
- **Space, at least partly.** Not one planet that reaches space only at the end. (F8)
- **Shounen ladder:** the big villain is guarded by charismatic lieutenants; beat them, beat him, then reveal a greater evil, and all former foes join the heroes. (F16)
- **Once he has shaped the structure himself, he accepts what follows from it.** The three-act outline was approved in one word, with the twist at about 7 hours in (not "in the middle", F7), a 3-hour Act 1 on Harrow (F8), and the "lights out" ending all flagged and all left alone. Don't expect him to reopen the consequences of structure he already set (cast F13-F15, villain ladder F16-F17). Early evidence (one data point): treat the "twist in the middle" and "space at least partly" lines as loose intent, not hard timing rules, unless he raises them again. Still check that a draft really is built from his own choices; new studio inventions still get tweaked. (F19, R14)
- **Climaxes are conquests, not quiet resolutions.** The final fight should be a full-on beatdown that the heroes win, with the hero becoming the god in a big way. This matches his earlier "fight an intergalactic god and become god" (F2) and "conquer a greater evil" (F16), and he turned the twist draft's soft finale into a "full on beatdown" (F20). Early evidence for the finale itself (one data point); the pattern is backed by F2 and F16. He then approved the revised twist, with the five-phase beatdown and the god-vs-god finale, as is: "D2: approved" (F21). That is a second data point that the beatdown finale is what he wanted. (F2, F16, F20, F21)
- **Keep pacing tight: no filler, and about 10 hours is a ceiling, not a target.** He approved the 10-hour structure (about 3 + 4 + 3 hours, two re-dressed moons, every studio addition kept) and added only "keep pacing tight" (F30). So the length is fine, but nothing should pad it: no stretched dungeons, no extra stages for their own sake, no grind. If a stage, fight or place does not move the story or teach or pay off something, cut it or shorten it. When in doubt, shorter. Also a game feel rule: the player should always be moving toward the next big thing (the climb, F2/F3). Not a conflict with "Escalating scale" (F2, F3): scale up fast, do not linger. Early evidence (one data point). Watch the two titan stages and the Tilly rematch first, since they are the most likely to read as padding. (F2, F3, F30)
- **Death and rebirth are themes, never mechanics, and never said out loud.** Understated, left for players to interpret. (F7)
- **Touchstones:** Gurren Lagann, The Matrix Reloaded, Dark Souls, Buddhist rebirth; FF7, MGS1, Fallout 3, Chrono Trigger, Chrono Cross, FF8, FF9, FFX, DQ7. Long cutscenes. Look of Mega Man Legends / Fear Effect (**update, F31, newer:** the look touchstones are now Mega Man Legends, Tail Concerto, MGS1 and FF7; Fear Effect is superseded, see Art direction). Inspired by, never copied. (F2, F31)

This is not a conflict with F11 ("too serious"): F11 was about the story taking itself too seriously, F21 is about what the groups are called. Read together: loud, punchy, military-sounding names, with goofy people inside them. Early evidence (one data point); the studio's earlier prediction that he would not comment on the wording was wrong (R16). Don't pick soft or cozy group names. **Update (F22, newer):** when the studio came back with hard names, he took them: Relay Watch Zero (the studio's recommendation, over First Mast Company and Iron Bell Battery), the toughen picks as written (Hardfall Corps, Scrapforge Division, Slipway Prime) and the HNS prefix, in four words ("D1 a d2 yes"). This is the first naming decision where he took the studio's pick. Once he has set a clear direction ("like Bravo Company"), he takes the studio's recommended execution of it. Early evidence (one data point). (F11, F21, F22)
- **The warm civilian name and the joke names can stay.** He did not push on any Keep, including Marchfolk (warm), Signals Corps, Rim Guard, the Overture, Coldrunners, or the joke ship and character names. So the rule is hard names for the military groups, with the humor left in the people and ships; warm names are not banned. This softens the "don't pick soft or cozy" line above: that line is about the groups that were rejected, not every name in the world. Early evidence (one data point, silence rather than an explicit yes). **Update (F23, newer):** he saw the kept names again in the glossary and approved as is, no renames: now an explicit yes, so two looks, no push. Still early evidence. (F11, F22, F23)
- **Small names: plain and punchy beats flashy.** He chose Clutch over the recommended Flashpoint (and over Showtime) for the timed-hit move (F25), and earlier renamed the hero "Red" (F15). He took the studio's pick for the gauge name (Juice), so the studio's recommendation is still a coin-flip on names: he follows whichever is the shorter, more everyday word. Early evidence (one data point on this; F15 is the second). Applies to game terms and character names, not to the hard military group names (F21), which are a separate rule. (F15, F21, F25)
- **The goofy tone held even inside the solemn twist.** He read the twist draft, with its sleeping crews and Red's mom, and said "tone is fine" (F20). Don't add extra solemnity or extra jokes there; keep the mix as drafted. Early evidence (one data point); it supports F11/F12. (F20)

"Forget" they're animals in the writing, never in the art. Note: F3 ("humanoids first and foremost") could be read as "not animals"; F5 is newer and clarifies they ARE animals. (F3, F5)
