# Tone Guide: grim world, loud heroes

> Owner: Creative Director (proposes) · Ross (approves). **TONE PASS 2026-10-08, pending Ross.** For the Writer, the artists and the Technical Artist. Spoiler-free: no twist content here.

> **For Ross:** One page that tells the Writer and the artists how your option A sounds and looks: grim world, loud heroes. It has five before/after lines from the current build, how Red's gestures read now, how the humor works, the games and films we borrow from (named openly, one job each), and a mood note for the look test. **Three calls for you are at the bottom.** One word, "approve", takes all three recommendations.

## The rule in one line
**The world is tired, hungry and watched. The crew is the light. Every joke either fights the darkness or shows how petty it is.**

Three layers, three voices:
- **The world (grim):** rust, grease, hunger, checkpoints, curfews, quotas, posters. People have mostly given up. They say less, flinch at noise, and their jokes are dry and short.
- **The villains (menace with a grin):** still loud, still funny, but they mean it. The joke is how ordinary they sound while they do awful things (Kasp's memos).
- **The heroes (loud):** exactly as written: brash, warm, defiant. In this world their loudness reads as courage. The game's arc is the world slowly getting louder around them.

## Red's silent gestures in this world
Red doesn't change. The world around her does, so the same gestures mean more.
- **Thumbs-up:** the only cheerful gesture anyone on Harrow has seen in years. NPCs react: a double take, a hand half raised, then dropped. Aimed at Vane or Kasp, it's the most defiant thing in the game. Never play it as irony or a wink.
- **Ears:** perked up means fired up, as before. Pinned flat now does two jobs: embarrassment with friends, anger at the Hegemony. Staging tells them apart.
- **Already running:** before, the joke was the empty room. Now it's who's left in it: someone who'd stopped expecting anyone to help.
- **Head shake:** only ever at the Hegemony or at giving up, never at someone asking for help.
- **The lamp check:** in a town where lamps cost a meal and get fined, a nightly ritual reads as quiet resistance. Stage it like one.
- **Legend correction** (three fingers, then two more, then a flex): still funny, and now it's the moment the bar laughs for the first time in the game.
- **Rule:** Red never mopes, never sighs, never shrugs at a call for help. Her silence reads as stubborn, not sad.

## How the humor lands now
- **Dry, short, earned.** Jokes come out of the bleakness: the absurd paperwork, the quotas, the posters, gallows lines from tired people. No mugging, no "Wheeee!", no cartoon sound effects in the world.
- **Up, never down.** Laugh at the system, the villains and the heroes' own swagger. Never at the beaten-down.
- **The crew is still loud.** Mox brags, Otis apologizes to enemies, Vela tickets everyone. The difference is that people around them flinch, then slowly start laughing along.
- **Villains: chilling-funny.** Same delivery, real consequences. Kasp sounds like an office manager and means every word: "MEMO. Effective tonight, grief is a noise."
- **The battle screen is the heroes' turf.** Ratings, flashes and K.O.! stay loud (see Call 2 for how they look).

## Borrowed, openly: one job each
Spirit and role borrowed; names, designs, dialogue and art are ours. Each source has one job, so nothing stacks into a copy.
- **FF7, Midgar's Sector 7 slums: the town.** A rusted, poor town in the shadow of an industrial power, with one warm bar where people still talk. That's Harrow Landing and The Long Wait.
- **Chrono Trigger, the 2300 AD domes: the townsfolk.** People who've stopped hoping and are just waiting, and a silent hero who walks in and moves anyway.
- **Half-Life 2, City 17: the occupation in the street.** Checkpoints, ID checks, the leader's face on every screen, guards who humiliate people because they can. That's the Signals Corps on patrol and Vane's broadcasts.
- **Brazil (the Terry Gilliam film): the paperwork.** Bureaucracy that's funny and horrifying at once. That's the noise tickets, the triplicate forms and Kasp's memos.
- **Fallout 3: the propaganda's voice.** Cheerful official messaging over a wrecked world. That's the posters ("A QUIET MOON IS A SAFE MOON") and the curfew siren playing Vane's fanfare.
- **MGS1, FOXHOUND: the villains.** Theatrical, eccentric bosses who talk to you mid-fight, are funny, and are truly dangerous. That's the Overture and Vane.
- **Blade Runner: the light.** Haze, harsh lights cutting through dirty air, small warm windows in a cold place. That's how we light Harrow.
- **Akira, Kaneda's gang: the crew's swagger.** Loud kids who don't care whose city it is, with their tags on every wall. That's the crew's attitude, MOX WUZ HERE, and the chalk lamps scrawled over Kasp's posters.
- **Gurren Lagann, the buried village: the hope curve.** A people kept underground and quiet, and loud heroes who punch through the ceiling and drag everyone up with them. That's the shape of the whole game.

## Visual mood (for the Technical Artist's look test)
Harrow should feel like a greasy machine town at the end of a long shift: everything rusted, oil-streaked and patched over, Hegemony posters half peeled over older Marchfolk paint, the ring-and-bar stenciled on every wall it owns, and chalk lamps and lanterns scrawled back on top by somebody who isn't giving up. Bring the fog closer and make it dirtier (brown-gray haze, not a clean night blue), push the contrast hard, and light the Hegemony's spaces with cold white floodlights and a slow searchlight at the checkpoint, hard-edged in the vertex lighting, so faces get lit from below. Warm amber stays the "ours" color from the style guide, but there's less of it: fewer, smaller lamps, so each one is precious and the eye goes straight to it. Marchfolk colors fade and go dusty; only the Hegemony's slate and white look clean, which is the point. The characters lose the toy sheen: no gloss spots, matte dusty fur, scuffed and stained clothes, leaner and a little taller, heavier brows over smaller eyes, while still clearly animals and readable in silhouette. Red's red jacket and her brass lamp should be the most saturated things in any shot after the window lamps: she's the light. For the battle, put the dock under floodlights with a drizzle if it's cheap, and keep the "!" cue and the ratings the loudest things on screen. Don't let it turn to brown mush: keep the grayscale check and the warm-ours / cold-theirs rule, and no gore.

## Ross's calls

DECISION NEEDED: Approve the tone pass, and how dry should the humor go?
Option A: **As written: chilling-funny.** The grim world and the loud crew as drafted; villain gags stay but get teeth (Dobb's billow, Kasp's cupholder and memos, forms in triplicate) — pros: keeps the characters you've approved, and the contrast makes the menace scarier; fewest rewrites / cons: some players may still read a few gags as light.
Option B: **Drier: menace first.** Same, but cut most villain and grunt gags (no billow, no cupholder brag, grunts don't grumble); the jokes live only in the crew — pros: the darkest read, the world feels most oppressive / cons: the villains lose a lot of their charisma, and their Act 3 crossover turns are harder to sell.
Option C: **Lighter: keep more of the old town humor.** Grim look and setting, but most current townsfolk lines stay goofy — pros: least rewriting / cons: the "too kiddy" feel you flagged comes back the moment anyone talks.
Recommendation: A. It's the "world grim, heroes loud" you picked, and villains who are funny and frightening at once (the MGS1 job) are the most memorable kind.

DECISION NEEDED: How many lamps are lit on Lamp Square at the start?
Option A: **Keep it as built: every window lit but one** (the dark window side job) — pros: no changes to the approved map; the lamps read as the one thing Harrow hasn't given up / cons: a brightly lit square works against "hopelessness".
Option B: **About half the windows dark; a few relight on the 30-second walk home after Kasp** — pros: you can see the hopelessness, and you can see Red's win bring it back; it also makes the end of Act 1, when every window on Harrow lights up at once, hit much harder / cons: small changes to an approved map (the side job becomes "the dark window above the store") and to a few lines; one light switch per window in data.
Option C: **Almost all dark; only Red's and a handful lit** — pros: the bleakest opening shot / cons: it fights the approved story (the lamp custom is the Marchfolk's symbol), and the dark-window side job stops making sense.
Recommendation: B. It turns "the heroes are the light" into something you can actually see on screen, inside the slice, for the price of a light switch.

DECISION NEEDED: How do the battle ratings (Nice! / Rad! / TOTALLY RAD!) look in the grim world?
Option A: **Keep them as they are:** bright candy colors, bouncy sign-painter letters — pros: maximum contrast; the heroes' voice is loudest in battle / cons: it's probably part of what read as "kiddy".
Option B: **Same words, tougher look:** spray-paint or stencil lettering, still hot orange, pink and gold but a little dirtier, and they still pop and bounce — pros: still loud, now in the same world (the Akira graffiti job) / cons: the style guide's lettering spec changes. The ratings are art row 37, which you haven't drawn yet, so nothing gets redrawn; the placeholders get a quick restyle.
Option C: **Drier words too** (for example "Clean." / "Sharp." / "PERFECT.") — pros: the most serious read / cons: loses the heroes' swagger in the one place they're meant to be loudest, and changes approved battle wording.
Recommendation: B. The heroes stay loud, the letters look like they belong on Harrow's walls, and it adds no new art.
