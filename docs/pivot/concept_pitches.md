# Pivot: Concept Pitches for a Single-Hero Action RPG

> Owner: Creative Director (proposes) · Ross (decides). Nothing here is final. Lights Left On, the turn-based version, is shelved, not deleted: every doc stays where it is.
> Date: 2026-10-08. Revised the same day after your note: "we can kind of keep the same characters and world but the psx look needs a upgrade to ps2". The Technical Director is checking what we can build and reuse, separately. This doc covers the creative side only.

---

## For Ross

You said the old game was "standard JRPG shit": predictable, grindy, the same old thing. You want Mega Man Legends' shape (one cool hero, a living town, dungeons full of loot, gear you can see on him, the helmet on and off) with controls that feel like Ninja Gaiden, Shinobi, Kingdom Hearts, FF7 Remake, DMC and Bayonetta: fast, powerful, mowing everything down, with swords that look badass when you upgrade them. Then you said to keep the characters and the world, and move the look up to PS2.

So all three pitches star **Red**, in **Harrow and the grim Marches**, opening on the **ore train**, through the **slums, the Spillway and the jammer works**. Otis, Mox, Vela and Ruo become the town, the workshop and the voices in your ear. The pitches differ in how the fighting feels, what the gear hook is and how the loop runs:

- **A. LIGHTS ON: the stylish all-rounder** (Kingdom Hearts, DMC, Bayonetta). A perfect dodge flares Red's lamp and slows every enemy in the glare. A style meter called Noise. Every piece of gear is a gift from a friend, so by the end you can see the whole crew on her. Harrow relights as you win.
- **B. REDLINE: the speed killer** (Shinobi, Ninja Gaiden). Red's lamp burns down unless she keeps fighting. Chain kills drop all at once. Otis forges her swords, and each one is a whole new fighting style. Harrow under curfew is a city you run through.
- **C. HULLBREAKER: the hunter** (Monster Hunter, FF7 Remake, Shadow of the Colossus). Stagger a big machine, climb it and rip a weapon off it. Mox forges what you tear off into gear that looks like what you beat. The Navy tows dead ships into Harrow's docks, and Red breaks into them at night.

**What I need from you (full versions at the bottom):**
1. Which version? I recommend **A**, borrowing C's "rip a weapon off a boss" for the big fights.
2. Which PS2 look leads? I recommend **stylized characters in a gritty world**: Jak II and Kingdom Hearts for the cast, Silent Hill 2/3 and MGS2 for Harrow's grime, DMC for the fight effects. It's your "characters pop off the grim world" note, one console generation up.
3. What next? I recommend **a one-room combat test you can play** before anyone rewrites the story. You judge feel in your hands.

Mix and match is welcome. The combat styles, gear hooks and loops can be swapped between pitches.

## 1. What made each reference fun

The job of each mechanic, and why it works. We borrow the job and give it a new face.

### Mega Man Legends (Capcom, 1998)
- **The town/dungeon loop.** Kattelox Island is one home town with ruins underneath and around it. Dive a ruin, come back up, something happens in town (an attack, a repair, a license test), and that opens the next ruin. You always know where home is, and home keeps changing.
- **The town feels alive.** People react to you, prices change with how you behave, and damage gets rebuilt when you donate. The town is somewhere you care about, not a menu with a map.
- **Materials and the workshop (Roll's role).** Bring parts home from the ruins and Roll builds new special weapons from them. Spend currency (refractor shards) to upgrade. Every dive pays off in something new on your arm: "I found a weird part" turns into "I have a drill arm now."
- **Visible gear.** The arm weapon changes shape, the boots change, and in Legends 2 the helmet comes on and off. Gear you can see makes every upgrade feel like a reward you can show off, not a number.
- **The recurring rivals.** The Bonnes, a charismatic pirate family, keep showing up with a new machine. The rivals are as fun as the hero.
- **What we leave out:** the tank controls.

### Ninja Gaiden Black (Team Ninja, 2005)
- **Every weapon is a whole fighting style.** Sword, staff, flails and a huge blade each have dozens of moves, and upgrading one at the shop adds moves and changes how it looks. Switching weapons changes how you play.
- **The loot is also the power.** Enemies drop glowing essence. Hold heavy attack and you pull it in to charge a big finisher (the Ultimate Technique). Picking up loot becomes a choice in the middle of a fight.
- **A signature finisher.** The Izuna Drop: launch, grab and piledrive. One move that becomes the hero's trademark.
- **Enemies fight back.** They are smart, fast and fair, so blocking, rolling and wall-running all matter. Winning feels earned.

### Shinobi (Overworks, PS2, 2002)
- **The hungry sword.** Hotsuma's cursed sword drains his life unless it keeps killing. You can't hang back and play it safe; the game pushes you forward.
- **Tate, the kill chain.** Kill several enemies in quick succession and they all die at the same moment in a short cinematic slash, with a damage bonus against bosses. You choose the order of the chain, so you build the power fantasy yourself.
- **Air-dash chains.** Dash from enemy to enemy through the air, using them as stepping stones over gaps. Speed is how you move and how you fight at once.
- **The scarf.** A long red trail behind the hero shows his speed and makes him readable in motion. A cheap visual with a huge payoff.

### Kingdom Hearts (Square, 2002; KH2, 2005)
- **One button that looks amazing.** Simple strings, air combos and lock-on: easy to start, flashy right away.
- **A new weapon from every world.** Each Keyblade keychain changes the blade's look and stats. "I got a new sword in this area and it looks super cool" is this exact feeling.
- **Reaction Commands (KH2).** One contextual button for a flashy counter on a specific enemy. Big moments without learning a new combo.
- **Drive Forms (KH2).** A temporary power-up that changes the outfit and lets you fight with two weapons. A transformation you can see.

### Final Fantasy VII Remake (Square Enix, 2020)
- **Pressure, then stagger.** Hit an enemy's weak point to pressure it, fill its stagger bar, and it collapses into a damage window that keeps growing. Every fight builds to a payoff.
- **Weapons teach skills.** Each weapon comes with a skill; use it enough and you keep it forever. Every new weapon is worth trying, even when it isn't stronger.
- **Weapon upgrade trees.** Small choices that make a weapon feel like your own.

### Devil May Cry (Capcom; DMC1 to DMC5)
- **Style ranks.** A letter grade from D up to SSS rises when you vary your attacks and drops when you repeat yourself or get hit. The game praises you for showing off, so you play more creatively.
- **Launcher and air juggles.** Knock an enemy up, jump after it, keep it in the air. Jump-cancelling and enemy-stepping (jumping off an enemy's head) keep the combo alive.
- **Weapon switching mid-combo (DMC4/5 Dante).** Swap weapons inside a combo without stopping. The skill ceiling is "how many tools can I chain together."
- **Devil Trigger.** A meter you fill by fighting and spend on a power mode. The super-powerful-being moment you earn.

### Bayonetta (Platinum, 2009)
- **Witch Time.** Dodge at the last instant and time slows for the enemies, not for you. The best reward in the genre for a good dodge.
- **Dodge Offset.** Hold attack through a dodge and the combo picks up where it stopped. Defending doesn't cost you your offense, so you stay aggressive.
- **Weapons on hands and feet.** Different weapons on your arms and legs give different moves. Your gear decides your move set.

### Three more worth borrowing
- **Sekiro (FromSoftware, 2019): parry as offense.** Perfect deflects fill the enemy's posture bar, and a full bar opens a deathblow. Sword clashes feel incredible, and parrying is how you win, not a chore. We take the parry and leave the slow, punishing pace.
- **Monster Hunter (Capcom): gear that shows what you beat.** Break parts off a big monster, carve materials, and forge gear that looks like it. Your outfit is a trophy case. We leave out the rare-drop farming, which is the grind you hate.
- **Ys VIII (Falcom, 2016): fast, friendly, with a growing village.** Flash Guard and Flash Move (a generous perfect guard and dodge with a slowdown) keep it fast and approachable. The castaway village grows as you rescue people, and each one opens a new service.
- *Honorable mentions:* Shadow of the Colossus (climbing a giant to find its weak spot), Ratchet & Clank (weapons that visibly level up), Jak II (an oppressed city under guard patrols you move through fast), Metal Gear Rising (cut a part off an enemy and take it).

## 2. The PS2 look (shared by all three pitches)

Your note: "the psx look needs a upgrade to ps2." What that means in plain terms:

**What goes:** wobbly, snapping vertices, warping textures, tiny blocky textures, the 320x240 chunky picture.

**What comes in:**
- **Smoother models.** A few thousand triangles for Red instead of a few hundred. Real fingers, a moving mouth, ears and tail that swing, a jacket that flaps.
- **Bigger, smoother textures** (roughly 256 to 512 pixels): readable patches, stencils, grime and rust.
- **A sharper picture**, around PS2 resolution (about 640x448), scaled up cleanly.
- **Glow and bloom** around lights: the soft halo of Ico, Shadow of the Colossus and Final Fantasy X. Red's lamp and the lit windows will sing with it.
- **Fog and haze** that hide the distance and make streets feel deep and dirty: Silent Hill 2 and 3.
- **Film grain and dirt** over the whole picture, also Silent Hill.
- **Sword trails and motion streaks**: DMC1 and 3, and Shinobi's scarf. Red's oversized jacket and her lamp leave trails.
- **Real shadows, sparks and light shafts** through slum grates and factory windows: MGS2's tanker and Big Shell, MGS3.
- **Your rules carry over:** the world stays desaturated, dark and grimy, and the characters and enemies stay a step brighter with an edge light, "not too bright" (your F56 note).

**The PS2 shelf, and what each gives us:**
- **Shinobi and DMC1/DMC3:** stark, graphic action on dark sets, red accents, trails. How fights should look.
- **Kingdom Hearts and Jak & Daxter / Jak II, Ratchet & Clank:** stylized, expressive heroes with big readable shapes, including animal and alien heroes. How the cast should look. Jak II's Haven City (a slum city under an army's boot) is the closest PS2 cousin to Harrow.
- **MGS2 and MGS3:** industrial detail, rain, wet metal, hard light. How the jammer works and the docks should look.
- **Ico and Shadow of the Colossus:** bloom, scale, giant things to climb. How the big machines should feel.
- **Final Fantasy X:** cutscene faces and staging at that fidelity. How story scenes should look.
- **Silent Hill 2 and 3:** fog, grain, rust and dark interiors. How the grime should feel.

**Cost to you, said plainly:** a PS2 asset is more work than a PS1 asset (more polygons, bigger textures, more animation). Red's locked shape (F43) doesn't need to change; she gets more detail, not a new design. CLAUDE.md's "The look" and docs/style_guide.md still say PSX. They get rewritten once you pick a look (decision 2), by the people who own them.

## 3. Three concept pitches (all Red, all the Marches)

**Shared across all three:**
- **Red is the only playable character.** Silent, as before, with a sword, the brass lamp at her hip, Mom's jacket and her goggles.
- **The crew are the town.** Mox and Otis fill the Roll workshop role (which one depends on the pitch). Vela is the voice on the radio in dungeons (Roll's radio role, and a bit of MGS's codec). Ruo runs the ship and black-market goods in Act 2.
- **The grim, greasy oppression stays:** checkpoints, Quiet Hours, propaganda, quotas, noise tickets.
- **The approved route stays:** ore-train opening, Harrow Landing's slums, the Spillway, the jammer works, the dead ore line, the curfew, then off-world.
- **The villain ladder is already built for this genre.** Kasp's eight legs to break, Tilly's net cannon to dodge, Brunt calling her punches (a parry boss), Calloway bolting on new arms mid-fight, Sorrell's duel, Vane attacking on the beat of his fanfare.
- **The twist and its clues are kept.** Each pitch notes what changes.
- **Gone:** turns, the party in fights, menus in fights, XP grinding.

### Pitch A: LIGHTS ON (the stylish all-rounder)

**Logline:** A silent shiba courier in her missing mom's oversized jacket cuts through an empire's grim frontier alone, with a sword, a brass lamp and every piece of gear her friends can weld onto her, to answer the distress call nobody else dares to.

**Gear hook: everything she wears is a friend's work, and you can see all of it.**
- **Goggles (the helmet toggle).** Down for fighting, up for town, or either one whenever you like. Our Mega Man Legends helmet. It's already her signature: she snaps them down before she runs.
- **Mom's jacket (armor).** Otis's dock crew rivet and stitch on patches, plates and linings, so the jacket gets more battle-worn as the game goes.
- **Gadget arm (Mox, the Roll role).** A gauntlet with a sub-weapon Mox builds from parts you bring him: a grapple, a rivet gun, a jammer-coil shock.
- **Boots.** Mox's hop-boots give the double jump, and upgrades add an air dash.
- **The sword.** One sword worth finding in every dungeon, and it looks like where it came from: a rebar cleaver from the Spillway, a humming jammer-antenna blade from the Works, a rail-spike sword from the dead ore line.
- **The lamp.** Watch Zero refits the housing, which changes the color and shape of its flare.
- **The idea underneath:** the old story's arc was "she learns to let people come along," shown by the whole crew in the shot beside her at the end. Now the crew is on her. By the last act, her silhouette is made of her friends' work.

**Look (PS2):** Kingdom Hearts and Jak II for Red's readable, expressive shape. DMC3 for fights that pop on dark sets. Silent Hill fog over Harrow, with bloom around her lamp so she's the light in every shot.

**Core verbs:** run, jump and double jump, dash with i-frames (a split second where hits can't touch you), light and heavy sword strings, a launcher and air juggles, lock-on, the gadget arm, and **Clutch** (your name for the timed press, now a perfect parry).

**Signature power: Porch Light and Noise.**
- **Porch Light** (Bayonetta's Witch Time, with our face on it). Dodge or Clutch at the last instant and her lamp flares. Every enemy in the glare is blinded and slowed for a couple of seconds while she carves through them.
- **Noise** (DMC's style meter). In a world where noise gets you ticketed, the style meter is how loud you are. Varied combos climb the spray-paint stencil ranks you approved (Nice!, Rad!, up to TOTALLY RAD!), with Signals noise tickets fluttering across the screen.
- **Lights On.** At the top rank, Red's lamp blazes, her edge light flares and she hits like a freight train for a few seconds. Signals grunts who see it wave the white flag and run, which is your EarthBound-fast surrender idea as an action moment.

**The loop:** Harrow Landing is home, like Kattelox. Dive a dungeon, come home with scrap and parts, and a town job opens the next one: Watch Zero's sit-in gets you to a gate, Vela forges checkpoint papers, Otis gets Biscuit running. Dark windows on Lamp Square relight, shops reopen and people come back outside, so you can see Harrow coming back to life (Mega Man Legends plus Ys VIII). In Act 2 the *Low Profile* is a moving home base between moons.

**Scale climb:** mostly fighting on top of bigger and bigger things: a walker's back, a titan's arm, a battleship's hull in flight (Bayonetta and DMC do this). About one short piloting set piece per act. The god finale stays.

**Why it's not "same old":** gear replaces levels, the town visibly comes back to life, and defense turns straight into offense.

**Twist:** kept whole. One new clue comes free: Red has no "call for help" button. No summons, no assist, in a genre that usually has them. It only makes sense the second time through.

**Cost to you:** modular gear for Red (about six slots, a few looks each across the game) and an action move set of roughly 40 to 60 animations.

**Risk:** the most familiar combat style of the three.

### Pitch B: REDLINE (the speed killer)

**Logline:** On a moon under curfew, the fastest courier on Harrow runs the slums, sewers and rails with a lamp that burns down unless she keeps fighting, and the only thing louder than the siren is her.

**Gear hook: the swords, forged by Otis (Ninja Gaiden's shop, Muramasa's role).** Otis turns the dock forge into a smithy. Every sword is a whole fighting style with its own move list, and every reforge adds moves and changes how it looks: a dock-chain sword that whips, a rail-spike blade that stabs and pins, a Signals antenna rapier that buzzes, a cargo-door cleaver that's barely a sword. Mox handles her boots: more air dashes, wall-run soles. The goggles toggle and the patched jacket carry over from A. Her too-big jacket is our Shinobi scarf: its tails and the lamp on its chain leave the speed trail.

**Look (PS2):** Shinobi's stark black-and-red action on dark sets. Jak II's Haven City for Harrow under curfew: patrols, searchlights, neon over grime. MGS2's rain on wet metal for the ore-train opening and the docks.

**Core verbs:** up to three air dashes (through enemies, Shinobi-style), wall run, rail grind along the ore line and the cable nests, light and heavy strings, a launcher, the **Drop** (an air grab and piledriver, our answer to the Izuna Drop), a perfect dodge, and lock-on. Offense first.

**Signature power: Lamp Oil and Curfew.**
- **Lamp Oil** (Shinobi's hungry sword, changed). Lamp oil is rationed on Harrow, and Red's lamp burns down when she isn't fighting. Kills and smashed Signals drones refill it. A bright lamp makes her faster and harder-hitting; a dim lamp makes her slow. It never drains her health; it only takes her speed. Hope runs on defiance, as a mechanic.
- **Redline.** With the lamp full, it burns white: everything doubles in speed, but the heat starts scorching her. Vent it in one big Porch Light finisher before it hurts too much.
- **Curfew** (Tate, our version). Cut through a chain of enemies and nobody falls until the chain ends. Then they all drop at once as her lamp flares. Longer chains give more oil and more damage against bosses.

**The loop:** Harrow is a city of districts under curfew, and Red is a courier. The courier office is the hub, and the dungeons are runs: the Spillway, the Works, the dead ore line, the Signals compound, each built as a fast vertical route with par times and medals (Bayonetta's grades, never a hard timer). Between runs you do the town thing that opens the next district: smuggle Watch Zero's bells, get Vela's papers, get the Coldrunners' route. As you clear runs, the curfew loosens street by street.

**Scale climb:** runs across moving freight, then on top of Biscuit, the titan and the ship.

**Why it's not "same old":** speed is the resource. The town is something you move through fast, not walk around in. Hard mode can be truly Ninja Gaiden hard.

**Twist:** kept whole. The lamp being fed by fighting quietly sets up its final payoff (the power that ends up inside it).

**Cost to you:** a large sword roster (each one needs its own move set, about 10 to 20 animations), and levels built for wall-running and grinding.

**Risk:** the hardest to tune, and it can turn away new players.

### Pitch C: HULLBREAKER (the hunter)

**Logline:** The Navy tows dead starships into Harrow's docks to strip them, and a courier with a sword breaks into them at night, tears weapons off the machines inside, and starts to wonder why every one of those ships came in with a lamp left on.

**Gear hook: weapons that look like what you beat, built by Mox (the Roll role, most literally).**
- **Rip it, then Mox forges it.** Tear a part off a big machine and Mox forges it into gear that looks like it (Monster Hunter): a crane-claw gauntlet, a turret-barrel spear, a saw-blade sword.
- **Two weapons at once, swapped mid-combo** (DMC5), each with its own moves.
- **Each weapon teaches a move** you keep once you've mastered it (FF7 Remake).
- **A salvaged Navy diving helmet** over her goggles for the airless ships (the helmet toggle, with a reason in the world). It comes off in town.
- **Mom's jacket** takes plates cut from the ships you dive.

**Look (PS2):** Silent Hill 2 and 3 for the derelicts' dark, rusted interiors, fog and grain. Shadow of the Colossus for climbing giant machines, with bloom on light through broken hulls. MGS2 for the Navy yard at night.

**Core verbs:** dash with i-frames, jump and double jump, light and heavy strings, a launcher and air juggles, lock-on, mid-combo weapon swap, a perfect guard, and stagger (FF7 Remake's pressure, then collapse).

**Signature power: Rip.** Stagger a big machine and Red climbs it (Shadow of the Colossus), tears a part off and fights with it for the rest of the fight: a turret arm as a cannon, a walker's leg as a hammer. It breaks after a while, but Mox can make it permanent. Crafting and the power fantasy happen in the same moment. (Monster Hunter's part breaks plus Metal Gear Rising's cut-and-take; Bayonetta lets you pick up enemy weapons too.)

**The loop:** the closest to Mega Man Legends: one home, many dives. Alongside the Spillway and the Works, the Navy keeps towing derelicts into Otis's dock yard (Otis hauled hulls for the Navy in the bible). Each new hull is a new dungeon, and each one is a different dead ship from a different century: a cathedral liner, a hospital barge, a racing yacht, a colony ark. Variety without a single forest or ice level. Town jobs get you in: Otis schedules the tow, Vela forges yard passes, the Coldrunners bid for what you bring out. Salvage you sell rebuilds Lamp Square.

**One reopened call, flagged:** in these dark hulls, Red's lamp would light the room around her (Silent Hill's flashlight). You said "sure that's fine" to the lamp not lighting rooms in the turn-based game. This pitch reopens that on purpose.

**Scale climb:** measured by what you can rip off: a walker's leg, then a titan's arm, then a battleship's railgun.

**Why it's not "same old":** no XP; your arsenal is the record of your game, and every boss fight can end with you holding its arm.

**Twist:** the same truth, pulled closer. Diving answerers' ships in Act 1 plants the clues early: a lamp painted by every bridge door, dead beacons, logs that all end the same way. It needs care, so the hulls read as ghost stories, not an early reveal. That's my job and the Writer's.

**Cost to you:** a ship kit re-dressed per era, and boss parts that work twice (on the boss and in Red's hands).

**Risk:** the deepest crafting loop, so the easiest to let slide into grind if we're careless.

## 4. Mechanics menu

Pick from it like a menu. "Core" means the game isn't fun without it. "Spice" means it adds a lot for a moderate cost. "Later" means after the first playable slice.

### Core
- **Camera-relative movement, free camera on the right stick.** The opposite of tank controls: push the stick and she goes that way.
- **Dash with i-frames.** A quick burst you can't be hit during; the backbone of feeling fast.
- **Jump and double jump.** Air control is half the fun in every reference you named.
- **Light and heavy strings.** Two buttons; mixing them in different orders gives different combos.
- **Launcher and air juggles.** Pop an enemy up, follow it, keep it airborne: the "super powerful being" feeling.
- **Lock-on.** Soft by default, hard lock on a button. Keeps fast fights readable.
- **One perfect-timing defense.** Either a parry (Clutch) or a perfect dodge with slow motion (Porch Light), with generous timing at first. This is the skill hook.
- **Visible gear slots.** Goggles/helmet, jacket, arm, boots and sword all change what's on screen. This is the Mega Man Legends promise.
- **Helmet (goggles) on/off toggle.** Cheap to build, and you called it iconic.
- **Materials and a workshop.** Around six to eight material types and one crafting friend. Bring stuff home, get something cool.
- **A new sword per dungeon that looks different.** The Kingdom Hearts keychain feeling.

### Spice
- **Style meter.** Ranks for varied play (DMC). Cheap to build, and it makes players show off.
- **Dodge offset.** Keep your combo through a dodge (Bayonetta). Defense doesn't kill your momentum.
- **Gear-driven move sets.** Each weapon or arm adds one to three moves of its own, so new gear plays differently, not just harder.
- **Weapon switching mid-combo.** Two weapons, one button to swap (DMC5). A huge skill ceiling; needs at least two finished weapons.
- **Stagger.** Pressure a weak point, then a big damage window (FF7 Remake). Bosses build to a payoff.
- **Kill chain.** A chain of enemies falls all at once (Shinobi's Tate). The best "mowing everything down" moment there is.
- **Power mode.** A meter spent on a few seconds of overpowered fighting (Devil Trigger, Drive Forms).
- **Sub-weapon or gadget arm.** The Mega Man Legends special weapon. Ranged options without making it a shooter.
- **Part breaking and ripping weapons off.** Tear pieces off big enemies and use them.
- **Fighting on giant things.** Boss fights across a moving titan or ship. Keeps the scale climb without a second control scheme.

### Later
- **Wall run and rail grind.** Great for speed, but every level must be built for them.
- **Enemy step and jump cancel.** Expert-level DMC tech; add once the basics feel right.
- **Grapple points.** Fast movement, but it changes level design.
- **Weapons that teach permanent skills** (FF7 Remake). Needs a big enough weapon roster to matter.
- **Piloting sections** (Biscuit, the titan, the ship). Each is a whole new control scheme; keep them rare.
- **Cinematic finishers, taunts, mission grades.** Polish.
- **A character creator.** Big art cost for you. Visible gear and paint give most of the feeling for a fraction of the work.
- **Hard mode with remixed enemies** (Ninja Gaiden Black's Master Ninja). For the second playthrough.

## 5. What NOT to do

The ways this could slide back into "standard" and "grindy."

- **No XP levels or stat grinding.** You get stronger from gear and skill. If an area needs grinding to pass, the area is wrong.
- **No rare-drop farming.** Bosses drop what you need for sure. No running a dungeon five times for a 3% drop.
- **No numbers-only gear.** If an upgrade doesn't change how Red looks or plays, it doesn't count as gear.
- **No menus in the middle of a fight.** Everything happens on buttons.
- **No damage-sponge enemies.** Small enemies die fast; big ones have tells and weak points.
- **No button-mash wins.** If mashing one button clears normal fights, enemies need to fight back harder (Ninja Gaiden's lesson).
- **No stamina bars or slow dodge rolls.** We want the speed you named, not Dark Souls' weight.
- **No forest, ice or fire dungeons.** Working places only: trains, slums, sewers, factories, ships, stations.
- **No fetch-quest towns.** Town jobs are short and story-driven, and the town looks different after.
- **No corridor of identical fights.** Every room adds a new enemy mix, a hazard or a shortcut.
- **No tank controls.** Camera-relative movement, always.
- **No overshooting PS2 into modern realism.** No photoreal faces, no glossy modern shine. Stylized and 2001-2005-era, grimy, edge-lit.
- **No piling on.** One borrowed role per part is fine. A part that matches one original in role, look, name and plot all at once gets changed until it's ours.

## 6. The choices

DECISION NEEDED: Which version of the Red action game do we develop?
Option A: LIGHTS ON, the stylish all-rounder (Kingdom Hearts, DMC, Bayonetta). Lamp-flare slow motion, the Noise style meter, gear given by the whole crew, Harrow relighting. Pros: easy to pick up and deep to master; uses four of the six games you named; the gear tells the story's heart. Cons: the most familiar fighting style of the three.
Option B: REDLINE, the speed killer (Shinobi, Ninja Gaiden). A lamp that burns down unless you fight, kills that drop all at once, Otis's forged swords, courier runs through curfew Harrow. Pros: the biggest speed and power rush. Cons: the hardest to tune, can turn away new players, and the sword roster costs the most animation.
Option C: HULLBREAKER, the hunter (Monster Hunter, FF7 Remake, Shadow of the Colossus). Stagger, climb and rip weapons off big machines, Mox forging them, dead ships towed into Harrow as dungeons. Pros: the most Mega Man Legends loop and the deepest gear system. Cons: the slowest fights, and it pulls twist clues earlier, which needs care.
Recommendation: A, with C's Rip added to the big boss fights. That gets the speed, the power and the "I tore this sword off that thing" moment, on the most readable base.

DECISION NEEDED: Which PS2 look leads?
