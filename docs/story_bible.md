## Logline
> 🟡 DRAFT: awaiting Ross's approval

Ten years after her mother flew into the starless Quiet to answer a distress call no one else would, scrap courier Pell Arden still lights a lamp in her window every night. When the Hegemony lands on her dust moon to silence the call for good, she goes to answer it herself, and has to fight an empire to get there.

## World & history
> 🟡 DRAFT: awaiting Ross's approval

**The Outer Marches.** The far edge of the galaxy, where the stars thin out. A frontier of dust moons, ring stations and trading ports: poor, windblown and stubborn. People patch what they own, live on scrap and salvage, and look out for their neighbors because nobody else will. Harrow, Pell's home, is one dust moon among hundreds.

**Jump lanes.** Ships travel between worlds on jump lanes: fixed, invisible routes that cross days of space in hours. Off a lane, travel is slow and dangerous. Pilots inherit their lane charts like family recipes. Whoever holds the lanes holds the Marches. The last lane ends at the Rim.

**The Hegemony.** The empire that rules the Marches from its shipyard capital, the Yards, a world wrapped in docks and slipways that never stop building. It brings order, roads and taxes, and backs all three with a navy. It is not cartoon evil: most of its troopers are moon kids who needed a wage.

**The Quiet and the call.** Past the Rim lies the Quiet, a starless dark where instruments drift, radios hiss to nothing, and ships that go in don't come back. Out of it comes the call: a faint distress signal anyone can catch on a cheap receiver pointed rimward. It asks for help in plain words and has never stopped. Old spacers swear it sounded different when they were young; most people say memory plays tricks. The Hegemony made answering it a crime, officially to stop people throwing their lives away.

**The lamp in the window.** On every frontier moon, families keep a lamp in the window for someone who went out into the Quiet. You light it every night, and you only put it out when they come home. Nobody alive has seen one put out for that reason.

**Where fantasy lives.** Sci-fi is the hardware; fantasy is the culture. Spacers carry charms, sing lane-songs to keep time through a jump, consult deck-side fortune readers, and talk about the Quiet like a haunted sea. The old relay masts on the dust moons, older than any records, are half ruin and half shrine. Everyone in the world is a chibi animal, and no one in the world ever remarks on it. Write them exactly as you would write people: species is never a joke, a trait, a power or a plot point.

**What the public knows (timeline)**
- **Before records:** The lanes, the relay masts, the call and the lamp custom are already there. No one knows how any of them started.
- **About 200 years ago:** The Hegemony spreads out from the Yards along the lanes and claims the Marches.
- **About 30 years ago:** Hegemony salvage crews start dragging derelict machines out of the edge of the Quiet: hulls of every make and age, some no shipyard recognizes. An officer named Sarrow Vane builds the modern navy around them.
- **About 25 years ago:** The Answering Ban. Crossing the Rim to answer the call becomes a crime.
- **10 years ago:** Pell's mother, a Harrow pilot, defies the ban and flies into the Quiet. Harrow still talks about it.
- **Now:** Hegemony crews begin fitting jammers to the old relay masts, starting with Harrow's.
