## Logline
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

Ten years after her mother flew into the starless Quiet to answer a distress call no one else would, scrap courier Pell Arden still lights a lamp in her window every night. When the Hegemony lands on her dust moon to silence the call for good, she goes to answer it herself, and has to fight an empire to get there.

## World & history
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

**The Outer Marches.** The far edge of the galaxy, where the stars thin out. A frontier of dust moons, ring stations and trading ports: poor, windblown and stubborn. People patch what they own, live on scrap and salvage, and look out for their neighbors because nobody else will. Harrow, Pell's home, is one dust moon among hundreds.

**Jump lanes.** Ships travel between worlds on jump lanes: fixed, invisible routes that cross days of space in hours. Off a lane, travel is slow and dangerous. Pilots inherit their lane charts like family recipes. Whoever holds the lanes holds the Marches. The last lane ends at the Rim.

**The Hegemony.** The empire that rules the Marches from its shipyard capital, the Yards, a world wrapped in docks and slipways that never stop building. It brings order, roads and taxes, and backs all three with a navy. It is not cartoon evil: most of its troopers are moon kids who needed a wage.

**The Quiet and the call.** Past the Rim lies the Quiet, a starless dark where instruments drift, radios hiss to nothing, and ships that go in don't come back. Out of it comes the call: a faint distress signal anyone can catch on a cheap receiver pointed rimward. It asks for help in plain words and has never stopped. Old spacers swear it sounded different when they were young; most people say memory plays tricks. The Hegemony made answering it a crime, officially to stop people throwing their lives away.

**The lamp in the window.** On every frontier moon, families keep a lamp in the window for someone who went out into the Quiet. You light it every night, and you only put it out when they come home. Nobody alive has seen one put out for that reason.

**Where fantasy lives.** Sci-fi is the hardware; fantasy is the culture. Spacers carry charms, sing lane-songs to keep time through a jump, consult deck-side fortune readers, and talk about the Quiet like a haunted sea. The old relay masts on the dust moons, older than any records, are half ruin and half shrine. Everyone in the world is a chibi animal, and no one in the world ever remarks on it. Write them exactly as you would write people: species is never a joke, a trait, a power or a plot point.

**What the public knows (timeline)**
- **Before records:** The lanes, the relay masts, the call and the lamp custom are already there. No one knows how any of them started.
- **About 200 years ago:** The Hegemony spreads out from the Yards along the lanes and claims the Marches.
- **About 30 years ago:** Hegemony salvage crews start dragging derelict machines out of the edge of the Quiet: hulls of every make and age, some no shipyard recognizes. An officer named Sarrow Vane builds the modern navy around them.
- **About 25 years ago:** The Answering Ban. Crossing the Rim to answer the call becomes a crime.
- **10 years ago:** Pell's mother, a Harrow pilot, defies the ban and flies into the Quiet. Harrow still talks about it.
- **Now:** Hegemony crews begin fitting jammers to the old relay masts, starting with Harrow's.

## Factions
> 🟡 DRAFT: awaiting Ross's approval

| Faction | What they want | Look / symbol | Role in story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hegemony Navy** (Admiral Sarrow Vane's fleet) | Control of every jump lane, peace and order on the frontier, and the Answering Ban enforced. Vane wants more: whatever lies at the heart of the Quiet, for his fleet. | Slate-gray armor with white trim, numbered helmets, Vane's officers in white greatcoats. Their ships are mismatched hulls of every shape, all painted the same gray. Symbol: a white ring cut by one straight bar (a slipway seen from above). | The main opposing force across all three acts. Lands troops on Harrow in Act 1, holds the blockade at the Rim in Act 2, chases the party into the Quiet in Act 3. Most troopers are moon kids on a wage, so fights against them should never feel gleeful. Where its salvaged machines came from matters (see RESTRICTED section). |
| **The Signals Corps** (Hegemony) | Clean, quiet airwaves: every relay mast jammed and the call silenced for good, under orders from the Yards. | Pressed blue-gray uniforms, earpieces, antenna pins on the collar. Symbol: a wavy line with a slash through it (a cut signal). | The Hegemony's listeners and jammers. In the slice, Sergeant Kasp's jammer crew is fitting the Old Relay Tower. Wren Tallow is one of theirs, and her defection at the end of Act 1 is the first crack in the Hegemony. Their crews have to listen to the call to jam it, and some of them don't come back from a shift quite the same. |
| **The Marchfolk** (frontier moon folk) | To be left alone: fair prices, free lanes, their customs kept, and their people home safe. | Patched work clothes, dust scarves, goggles, charms on every belt and bulkhead. Symbol: the lamp in the window. | The people the game is about. Harrow Landing is the slice town. They're split: many have sons and daughters in Hegemony uniform and keep their heads down, while others quietly help Pell. Their trust grows moon by moon in Act 2 as word of her spreads. |
| **The Coldrunners** (smugglers and lane-runners) | Profit, unpatrolled routes, and their family lane charts, including side-lanes no Hegemony map shows. | Dark coats, running lights painted over, ships that fly "cold" with engines low to slip past patrols. Symbol: a shuttered lantern, chalked on crates and hatches. | The outlaw way around the Hegemony. Pell's courier jobs brush up against their crates in the slice. In Act 1 they're the only way off a locked-down Harrow, and they might help her or sell her out. In Act 2, Ruo Vantell and his crew give the party a ship to the Rim. Some Coldrunners will fly an answerer toward the Rim, for a steep price. |
| **The Tenders** (keepers of the relay masts) | The old masts left standing and unjammed, the call heard, and the old customs kept: lane-songs, charms, lighting the lamps. | Dust-colored wraps, small bells sewn to their sleeves, brass ear-cups for listening. Symbol: a mast with three rings around its top. | A small, aging order who look after the half-ruined masts like shrines. In the slice, Harrow's Tenders protest the jammer and show Pell the way into the Old Relay Tower. They keep hand-copied records of the call going back further than anyone else's (see RESTRICTED section). |

In the vertical slice, the Signals Corps is jamming Harrow's mast, the Tenders are protesting it, and most Marchfolk keep quiet because the Hegemony pays their kids' wages. Pell is caught in the middle: a courier who runs the odd Coldrunner crate and lights a lamp the Tenders would bless. In Act 1 the Navy locks Harrow down, which forces the Marchfolk to pick a side, leaves the Coldrunners choosing between selling Pell out and sneaking her off-world, and opens a crack in the Signals Corps when Wren stops jamming the call and starts listening to it.
