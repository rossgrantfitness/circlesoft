## Logline
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (tone pass; names updated)

Ten years ago, scrap courier Red Kincaid's mom roared off into the starless Quiet to answer a distress call nobody else had the guts to touch. Now the Hegemony wants that call shut up for good, so Red is going to answer it herself, and if an entire empire wants to stand in her way, that's honestly the empire's problem.

## World & history
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (tone pass; names updated)

**The Outer Marches.** The ragged edge of the galaxy, where the stars thin out and the people get tougher. Dust moons, ring stations, rowdy trading ports. Everybody's broke, everything's held together with tape, and anybody will lend you a wrench. Harrow, Red's home, is one dust moon out of hundreds, and by local accounts the loudest.

**Jump lanes.** Invisible highways that cross days of space in hours. Flying off-lane is slow and a terrible idea. Pilots hand down lane charts like secret family chili recipes. Whoever holds the lanes holds the Marches. The last lane ends at the Rim.

**The Hegemony.** The empire running the Marches from Slipway Prime, a shipyard world wrapped in docks that never stop building. It brings roads, order, taxes and a truly heroic amount of paperwork, and backs it all with a navy. Its admirals give speeches. Its grunts mostly want lunch.

**The Quiet and the call.** Past the Rim lies the Quiet: a starless dark where compasses spin and radios hiss. Ships go in; none have come out. Yet. Out of it comes the call, a distress signal any cheap receiver can catch, asking for help in plain words, nonstop, forever. Old spacers swear it sounded different when they were young; everyone else rolls their eyes. The Hegemony made answering it a crime "for safety," which instantly made it the coolest crime on the frontier.

**The lamp in the window.** Families with someone out in the Quiet light a lamp in the window every night until that someone comes home. Nobody's ever gotten to blow one out. Red plans to be first.

**Where fantasy lives.** Sci-fi is the hardware; fantasy is the culture. Spacers wear lucky charms, belt out lane-songs through every jump, swear by deck-side fortune readers, and trade Quiet ghost stories at the bar. The relay masts on the dust moons, older than any records, are half ruin, half shrine. Everyone is a chibi animal and nobody ever mentions it: species is never a joke, a trait, a power or a plot point.

**What the public knows (timeline)**
- **Before records:** The lanes, the relay masts, the call and the lamp custom are already there. Nobody knows who started any of it.
- **About 200 years ago:** The Hegemony rolls out from Slipway Prime and plants its flag on the Marches.
- **About 30 years ago:** Salvage crews start hauling derelicts off the edge of the Quiet: hulls of every make and age, some no shipyard recognizes. A rising officer named Sarrow Vane bolts the modern navy together out of them and paints the whole mess gray.
- **About 25 years ago:** The Answering Ban. Crossing the Rim to answer the call becomes a crime.
- **10 years ago:** Red's mom, Harrow's hottest pilot, defies the ban and blasts off into the Quiet. Harrow still tells the story, and it gets bigger every time.
- **Now:** Hegemony crews start bolting jammers onto the old relay masts, starting with Harrow's. Big mistake.

## Main cast
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

Red, Otis and Mox are the vertical slice party. Vela joins at the end of Act 1; Ruo joins in Act 2. Red never speaks, so the other four carry every conversation, and each one "translates" her differently: Mox narrates what she's obviously thinking (always wrong, always heroic), Otis reads her right with one glance, Vela logs her nods as official statements, and Ruo scores her silences for style.

### Red Kincaid
- **Role:** Hero, silent protagonist, and the party's quick front-line fighter. A scrap courier on Harrow who has lit her mom's window lamp every single night for ten years. Playable from the first second of the game (slice party). She never speaks a line of dialogue: the party and NPCs do the talking, and Red answers with her face, her ears, her fists and her feet. Default name **Red Kincaid**; the player can rename her at the start. Usually just called **Red**. Gender: female (she/her), which keeps the approved Logline, World and Factions text as written.
- **Look:** Chibi dog, a scrappy mutt pup with tawny-red fur, a cream muzzle and chest, and a short tail that wags whether she wants it to or not. Her ears are her trademark: one stands straight up, one flops over, and the up-ear perks even higher when she's fired up. Wears her mom's beat-up red flight jacket, three sizes too big, sleeves rolled into fat cuffs, with Harrow patches and a hand-painted lamp on the back. Goggles pushed up between the ears, courier satchel, scuffed boots. Signature item: a heavy brass hand lamp on a short chain clipped to her belt, always at her hip. Weapon: a scavenged sword, a little too big for her, carried over her shoulder. Silhouette: one ear up, giant jacket, sword on her shoulder, little lamp swinging at her hip.
- **Personality:** Stubborn hope at full volume, without a single word: she would rather be wrong and moving than right and waiting. She's all big grins, bigger swings and zero hesitation, and she is the most loyal person on the frontier. If somebody asks for help, she has already started running.
- **Battle style:** Quick front-line attacker who fights with her sword and acts early in the turn order; nail the timed press on her swings and they hit harder. Signature move **Porch Light**: she flares the brass lamp at her hip to blind the enemy, and a well-timed press adds a full-spin sword slash on top.
- **Arc:** Red sets out answering one call for one person, and over the journey ends up fighting for every lamp-lit window in the Marches. She learns that hope isn't just refusing to quit; it's letting people come along with you. Because she can't say it, the staging says it: early cutscenes frame her alone, out in front; by the end, the whole crew is in the shot beside her. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **How they show it:**
  - *The lamp check.* Every night at the window (also her rest/save animation), she lights the brass lamp, taps the glass twice with a knuckle, and gives the dark sky a big thumbs-up, like somebody out there can see it.
  - *Already running.* Whenever anyone asks for help, she doesn't nod and she doesn't wait: before they finish the sentence, she snaps her goggles down and the camera cuts to her already halfway out the door. The NPC finishes the sentence to an empty room.
  - *Legend correction.* When a barfly tells a wildly exaggerated tale of her exploits ("she beat fifty troopers with one paw!"), her up-ear twitches, and she holds up three fingers to set the record straight... then thinks about it, holds up two more, and flexes.
- **Alternates for Ross:** **Cat:** a scrappy tuxedo cat with white socks, a crooked tail tip and the same giant red jacket. · **Tiger cub:** a round orange tiger cub with bold black stripes, chunky paws and little round ears, jacket and goggles unchanged.

### Otis Kettle
- **Role:** Tank, and later the crew's walker pilot. Harrow's dock boss and the closest thing the docks have to a dad. Joins in the slice when Sergeant Kasp's crew starts shoving his dockhands around (slice party). At the end of Act 1, Red "borrows" his cargo walker, Biscuit, to bust off Harrow, and Otis comes along to make sure nobody scratches the paint.
- **Look:** Chibi brown bear, big and round, with a grizzled gray muzzle and small round ears poking through two holes cut in his hard hat. Faded orange dockworker coveralls, a padded work vest, the hard hat with a little headlamp, thick work gloves, and a dented tin of cough drops in his chest pocket. Signature item: a dented cargo-container door with a handle welded on, used as a shield. Weapon: a big dockworker's hammer in his other paw. Silhouette: a friendly boulder carrying a door and a hammer.
- **Personality:** The gentlest soul on Harrow: he hums old work songs, calls everybody "friend," and never, ever raises his voice, even while lifting a trooper over his head. Quirk: he politely apologizes to every enemy before he flattens them, then tucks a cough drop in the pocket of anyone he knocks out. Every night he counts his dockhands off shift by name and won't lock up until the count comes out right. He used to pilot for the Navy and walked off one day without explanation; at heart he believes every crew deserves someone waiting for them on the dock. He's the one person who reads Red perfectly, and usually the one telling Mox to sit down.
- **Battle style:** Tank who swings his hammer, soaks hits and can stand guard over an ally with his door shield; when the guarded ally gets attacked, a good block from Otis takes the whole hit for them. Signature move **Heave-Ho**, picking up one enemy and setting it down, very firmly, on another; a well-timed press makes the landing extra firm.
- **Arc:** Otis has spent years keeping his head down and his dock quiet, and the journey asks him to stand up and get loud for something. He's the kettle that never whistles, until the one time he raises his voice in the whole game, and it should rattle the speakers. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Sorry about this, friend. Nothing personal. Mostly structural."*
  - *"A dock is just a long wait with a roof on it. Somebody has to stand at the end of it."*
  - *"No, son, that look does not mean 'unleash the prototype.' That look means 'put the prototype down.'"*

### Mox
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

- **Role:** Mechanic and gadget fighter; the crew's engineer. A Harrow dock kid who joins in the slice by stowing away in Red's delivery crate on the run to the Old Relay Tower (slice party). Red tries to send him home several times. It never works.
- **Look:** Chibi ferret kid, long and wiry, with a welding mask flipped up on his head like a crown. An oversized utility vest with far too many pockets, each one bulging with something, plus a wrench nearly as tall as he is, welded up into a mace (his weapon). A small, wobbly homemade drone named Tuesday (built on a Tuesday) buzzes over his shoulder. Every gadget he builds is welded with "MOX WUZ HERE." Silhouette: mask, wrench, pockets, drone.
- **Personality:** Brags nonstop about inventions that are about forty percent finished. Quirk: he panics exactly once per crisis, loudly, for about four seconds, then gets to work, so the crew has learned to just wait it out. Underneath the boasting is a kid who has never been off Harrow and is terrified of being left behind, which is exactly why he keeps climbing into crates.
- **Battle style:** Bonks enemies with his wrench-mace and pulls random gadgets out of random pockets: traps, buffs, repairs and the occasional small explosion. Every gadget has its own wild timing mini-press (tap on the beep, hold until the light goes green, mash before it overheats), and nailing it makes the result bigger. Signature move **Patent Pending**, an untested contraption with a wild (and occasionally amazing) result; the better the press, the better the odds.
- **Arc:** Mox starts as a stowaway chasing glory and grows into the engineer who keeps every walker, titan and ship running when it matters most. Along the way he finds out the crew was never going to leave him behind, and he stops bragging about what he'll build and starts building it.
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Stand back, everybody, I've done this a hundred times! ...Once. I've watched somebody do it once."*
  - *"You can tell me to go home. I'm just gonna get in the next crate."*

### Vela Quist
- **Role:** Healer and support. A Signals Corps listening specialist assigned to jam the call, who has to listen to it to do her job, actually listens, and at the end of Act 1 quits in style mid-battle by handing over her resignation form, in triplicate, then switching sides on the spot.
- **Look:** Chibi bunny: a small, round rabbit with soft silver-gray fur, a fluffy cotton tail and two tall ears that she keeps clipped neatly together, the smallest member of the crew by a lot. Pressed blue-gray Signals Corps uniform (she keeps it pressed even after she quits, with the Corps patch swapped for a hand-stitched lamp), giant headphones clamped over the base of her ears with the tall tips poking up through the headband like antennas, and an antenna pin on the collar. Signature items: a clipboard nearly as big as she is and a rubber stamp she wields like a holy relic, mounted on top of a tall brass staff (her weapon; a healer's staff with a stamp for a head). Silhouette: a round little bunny with tall ears sticking out of giant headphones, clipboard raised like a shield. Her ears give her away: they droop when she's flustered and shoot straight up when she's about to break a rule.
- **Personality:** Prim, exact, and speaks seven languages, none of them casually. Quirk: she still writes noise tickets out of pure habit, including for Otis's humming, Mox's explosions and her own heroic war cries. Red is the only member of the crew who has never earned one, which Vela finds deeply suspicious, so she has started logging Red's nods, shrugs and thumbs-ups as formal statements for the record. At her core she is someone who heard a stranger asking for help, and could not unhear it, and that was the end of her career.
- **Battle style:** Healer and support who heals, shields and cures; her stamps land in rhythm, and each stamp pressed on the beat heals a little more. Signature move **Approved in Triplicate**, three loud stamps that patch up the whole party, with a bonus if all three land on the beat.
- **Arc:** Vela begins certain that following the rules is the same thing as doing good, and breaks the biggest rule on the frontier because listening was the right thing to do. Her careful notes on the call become one of the crew's most important tools. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Under regulation 14-C, I'm required to warn you before I do this. Consider yourself warned. Twice. Once more for my records."*
  - *"They told me to listen so I'd know what to silence. I listened. Somebody needs help. That's the whole message."*
  - *"Let the record show Red nodded. Let the record also show she was out the door before I finished the question. Amending the record."*

### Ruo Vantell
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

- **Role:** Fast attacker and thief; Coldrunner captain and the crew's ship. Joins in Act 2 when the party needs a ride to the Rim, flying his ship, the *Low Profile* (the loudest ship ever to fly cold).
- **Look:** Chibi raccoon with a dramatic striped tail and a mask that looks like he was born for this job. A long dark Coldrunner coat with the running-light patches painted over, a scarf he flips over his shoulder at every opportunity, and fingerless gloves. Signature items: a shuttered lantern on his belt (the Coldrunner symbol) and twin flare pistols, saved for his signature move. Weapon: a pair of long Coldrunner knives. Silhouette: long coat, big tail, a lantern swinging behind him.
- **Personality:** Runs blockades, flirts with danger, and has a reputation even worse than his manners. Quirk: true to Coldrunner tradition, he scores everything out loud for style, from getaways to the crew's breakfast to his own entrances (always a ten). His big brother flew rimward to answer the call eleven years ago, and Ruo keeps a lamp lit in his cockpit that he swears is just for reading.
- **Battle style:** Fast attacker with quick twin-knife strikes, item steals and showy finishers. Signature move **Ten Out of Ten**, a flare-pistol volley that adds another shot with each good press, up to a perfect ten (and Ruo scores the result out loud).
- **Arc:** Ruo signs on claiming it's strictly for the fee, and spends Act 2 slowly admitting he was always going to the Rim anyway. The smuggler who has spent his whole life finding ways around things finally decides to go straight through. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"That was a six. Sloppy landing, no flair. Watch me, I'll show you a ten."*
  - *"I've been flying the long way around the Rim for eleven years. Turns out I was just waiting for somebody stubborn enough to go straight."*

## Villains
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (species updated per Ross)

**The ladder.** The villains grow right alongside the party: Sergeant Kasp is the slice boss, fought on foot at the Old Relay Tower, and Tilly Ransome hounds Red from the Act 1 escape onward. Admiral Vane is guarded by his three lieutenants, the Overture, and the party beats them one at a time, each in a bigger machine: Lieutenant Brunt at the Act 1 finale during the Harrow breakout (Biscuit, big robot), Lieutenant Calloway in the middle of Act 2 (the stolen titan, giant robot), and Lieutenant Sorrell at the Rim blockade late in Act 2 (the titan against a titan built onto a battleship). With the Overture down, Vane comes for Red himself at the end of Act 2, his flagship against the party's giant spaceship, and the moment he loses, the game turns over: the real enemy (see RESTRICTED section) takes the stage, and it is far bigger than any admiral. Act 3 is the giant team-up, where every foe the party has beaten (the three lieutenants, Vane, Kasp and Tilly) crosses over for the final campaign against the real enemy, the last step of the climb from on foot to god. Every boss plays with timed hits in its own way, and every one of them walks away from their fight owing Red something, so when they switch sides it lands as a payoff, not a surprise.

### Admiral Sarrow Vane
- **Role:** Main villain of Acts 1 and 2, commander of the Hegemony Navy, the man who built it out of salvage, and the top rung of the ladder. Mostly a voice on broadcasts and a face on posters in the slice; arrives on Harrow in person in Act 1 with maximum fanfare to lock the moon down (then leaves Lieutenant Brunt to finish the job); sends his lieutenants after Red one at a time through Act 2; and once the Overture has fallen, comes for her himself at the end of Act 2. After his defeat and the reveal, he joins the Act 3 team-up, bringing what's left of his fleet and all of his ego.
- **Look:** Chibi white lion: round and short like everyone else, with an enormous silver mane combed back into big dramatic swoops. A white Navy greatcoat with gray trim, shoulder boards far too wide for him, and a hem that drags on the floor unless someone is billowing it. A plumed white bicorne hat sitting on top of the mane. Signature item: a slim white conductor's baton he uses to cue his own fanfare, which doubles as his fleet command rod. Always one step behind him: Ensign Dobb, a nervous junior officer with a hand-crank bellows whose whole job is the billow. Silhouette: a huge mane, a huge hat and a coat flaring out behind a very small admiral.
- **Motive:** Thirty years ago Vane built a navy out of hulls dragged off the edge of the Quiet, and he has wanted the place they came from ever since: whatever lies at its heart, for his fleet, and a statue of himself next to it, visible from orbit. Officially he silences the call "for safety." Privately, it is the only broadcast in the Marches louder than his speeches, and he cannot stand the competition. He wants to be the one name the whole frontier remembers forever.
- **Personality:** A born showman who treats every room like an opera house: monologues, encores, and dramatic pauses long enough to make his officers check their watches. Quirk: he cannot begin anything, including a sentence, until his fanfare finishes, and he restarts it if anybody interrupts. The edge is under the sequins: he is genuinely brilliant at fleets, he never forgets a slight, and he spends crews like pocket change, because to Vane everyone else is either audience or scenery.
- **Boss fight:** End of Act 2, ship against ship. Rig: the *HNS Magnificent*, his flagship, a gray colossus of mismatched hulls wearing a white greatcoat the size of a sail (Ensign Dobb now runs a very large bellows). He conducts the battle with his baton in three movements, each one a louder remix of a lieutenant's trick (called shots, bolt-on arms, duelling stances), so the whole ladder turns out to be training for this fight. His baton conducts his fanfare, and the fanfare is the tell: every attack lands on the beat, so the party reads the rhythm to time its blocks, and the tempo speeds up with each movement. Each movement ends with his "Grand Finale," a huge multi-hit barrage that lands on the fanfare's closing beats; block every hit in rhythm to get through it, miss the beat and it hits like a battleship.
- **Arc:** Vane starts the game as a punchline in a fancy coat and, across Acts 1 and 2, becomes the most dangerous person in the galaxy: every time Red embarrasses him in public, he stops performing a little more and starts hunting her for real, and he spends his own lieutenants like pocket change to do it, which is exactly why he loses them one by one. By the end of Act 2 the jokes have run out and only the threat is left; he would rather own the dark than let anyone come home through it. Then he loses, the real enemy (see RESTRICTED section) steals the spotlight, and Sarrow Vane meets the one thing he hates more than competition: being upstaged. He joins the Act 3 team-up for reasons that are about ninety percent vanity, and his showmanship turns out to be gloriously useful: his fanfare becomes the signal for the whole combined fleet to charge, his speeches rally crews who were shooting at each other last week, and nobody alive is better at making a hundred mismatched ships move like one orchestra. He never quite apologizes. He does, once, let someone else take the bow. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Strike the fanfare! No, the long version. The one with the choir. Ensign Dobb: billow."*
  - *"Keep your little lamp, courier. Shine it all you like. I own the dark you're pointing it at."*
  - *"Fleet! Follow the coat! ...Courier. After you. Do not get used to it."*

### The Overture (Vane's three lieutenants)
Nobody gets to the Admiral without getting through his opening acts first, and Vane introduces each one like the next movement of a symphony. Each runs a different branch of the Hegemony, pilots a bigger machine than the last, and is charismatic in a completely different way: Brunt is the crowd's champion, Calloway is the happiest person in any room with a toolbox in it, and Sorrell could talk a blockade into letting itself through. All three are full Lieutenants, which Sergeant Kasp brings up constantly. *Alternates for Ross:* group name **the Opening Acts** or **the Encore**; species swaps: koala for Brunt, hamster for Calloway, red panda for Sorrell.

### Lieutenant Hollis Brunt
- **Role:** First lieutenant of the Overture and the Act 1 finale boss. Commander of the Hardfall Corps, the Hegemony's ground troops: the grunts in numbered helmets. Vane drops her on Harrow to run the lockdown, and she meets the party at the climax of the breakout, Red in Biscuit against her walker. Recurs in Act 2 demanding rematches; joins the Act 3 team-up with every grunt who will follow her, which is all of them.
- **Look:** Chibi rhino: stocky, broad and low to the ground, round slate-gray hide, little round ears, and one short, blunt nose horn wrapped in white boxing tape to match her fists, like a third glove she leads with. Slate-gray Hardfall Corps armor with the sleeves torn off, taped-up fists, and her regulation white officer's greatcoat cut down into a short ring cape because she kept tripping on it. A numbered helmet (with a hole punched in the front for the horn) hangs off her belt, never worn ("they need to see the face"). Signature item: a dented trophy cup she brings to every fight "for the winner," and has never once had to hand over. Silhouette: a low, wide brick with a taped horn, a cape and a trophy.
- **Motive:** Her troops and a fair fight. Hollis came up from the ranks, knows every grunt by name (even when they don't know their own number), and believes the Hegemony is worth serving as long as it fights clean and brings its people home. Beating the most famous courier on the frontier in front of everybody wouldn't hurt either.
- **Personality:** A hot-blooded champion who treats every battle like a title match and every crowd like it came to see her. Quirk: she calls her shots out loud ("Left hook! The bear! Next turn!") and has never missed one. Her code: no hits from behind, no hits on anyone who's down, and no punch she hasn't called first. The edge: knowing where the punch is coming from doesn't help much when it's her punch; she's the strongest fighter in the Navy, and her troops would follow her into a sun.
- **Boss fight:** Act 1 finale, Biscuit against the *HNS Title Shot*, a heavy hauler walker rebuilt as a boxer, with huge padded fists and a ring of floodlights on its shoulders. Each turn she calls her next target and punch out loud ("Right cross! The bear! Next turn!"), so the party can heal up or put Otis's shield in the way. Every called punch is telegraphed with a big wind-up, so the target can time a perfect block that shrugs off the hit and earns a counter-jab; miss the timing and it lands like a freight hauler. She never hits anyone who's down. Her troopers in the stands cheer her stronger every round; knock out the cheering section (they wave white flags and leave quietly) to keep her from getting pumped up. In the last phase her own troopers start throwing uncalled cheap shots at the party, and she stops mid-fight to chew them out.
- **Arc:** Hollis loses the breakout and is thrilled about it: finally, a real opponent. When the *HNS Title Shot* topples, Red swings Biscuit around to catch it before it lands on Hollis's own cheering troopers, and Hollis never forgets it. Through Act 2 she turns up for rematches nobody scheduled, and watches Vane spend her Hardfall Corps like pocket change.   - *"Get up, courier! I did not cross three moons to win by forfeit!"*
  - *"Hardfall Corps! Helmets on, numbers optional! We've got a title shot!"*

Chief Engineer of the Scrapforge Division on Slipway Prime, the branch that turns hulls dragged off the edge of the Quiet into the Hegemony's navy. She built the prize titan the party steals in Act 2, and the moment they steal it, she comes after them in something bigger. Joins the Act 3 team-up as the crew's second engineer, and Mox's favorite (and least favorite) person.
- **Look:** Chibi heron: a round, fluffy blue-gray body with almost no neck, a short stubby yellow beak, and a single crest feather at the back of her head that curls up like a question mark. Her two long, thin legs (simple sticks with knobby knees) make her the tallest of the Overture and give her a jittery, stilt-walking bounce. A white Scrapforge lab coat three sizes too big, scorched at the hem and flapping around her shins, sleeves rolled up her wings over a welder's apron, and goggles with a dozen flip-down lenses perched above her beak, usually all flipped down at once. Silhouette: a fluffy ball in a big coat on two long stilts, crest curled, stacked goggles, label-maker raised.

For thirty years Bix has rebuilt hulls nobody can explain, a few of which even she can't figure out, and Vane has never once let her past the Rim to see where they came from. The party wins by scrapping arms faster than she can bolt them on.
- **Arc:** Bix loses her prize titan and her own robot in the same week and could not be happier: she has never had test pilots this good. For the rest of Act 2 the party keeps finding notes from her tucked into the stolen titan's maintenance hatches, addressed to whoever's fixing it ("Coolant valve sticks. Kick it. Gently. Love, B."), and she and Mox become pen pals who refuse to admit it. When Vane orders the stolen titan scrapped by remote rather than let rebels keep it, she "loses" the code. Once the real enemy shows itself she crosses over in a heartbeat: it's the biggest engineering question in the history of the galaxy, and she was never going to let anyone else answer it.
- **Sample lines:**

- **Role:** Third lieutenant of the Overture and the late-Act 2 boss. Commander of the Rim Guard, the blockade at the end of the last jump lane, where every answerer gets turned back. The last wall between Red and the Quiet, and the last door before Vane. Joins the Act 3 team-up.
- **Look:** Chibi spotted hyena: sandy-tan fur dappled with dark spots, a dark muzzle, big round ears, a short bristly mane down the back of her neck that she combs into an immaculate crest, and a permanent half-smile with just a hint of teeth. A tailored white Rim Guard dress uniform with gold frogging, a short duelling cape thrown over one shoulder, mirror-polished boots, and one white glove on (the other always in hand, ready for a challenge). Signature item: a slim rapier with a ring-shaped hand guard cut by a single bar, the Hegemony symbol. Silhouette: big round ears over a combed crest, a flared half-cape, a rapier raised in salute.
- **Motive:** Style, and the line. For nine years Celestine has stopped every ship that tried to cross the Rim, politely, beautifully, with a bow, and nobody has ever gotten past her. Vane told her the blockade keeps people safe, and she believes it with her whole heart. Secretly, she's been waiting nine years for someone worthy enough to beat her, and has started to suspect it will never happen.
- **Personality:** The smoothest talker in the Navy: she compliments her opponents, flirts with danger, has a quotation ready for every occasion, and has charmed more Coldrunners into turning around than she has ever had to fight. Quirk: she narrates her own duels in elegant play-by-play and applauds a good hit even when it lands on her. Ruo once scored one of her interceptions a ten and has regretted it ever since. The edge: she's the finest fencer in the galaxy, the blockade under her is airtight, and the charm is velvet over a very hard wall.
- **Boss fight:** Late Act 2 at the Rim blockade, the stolen titan against the *HNS Riposte*: a slender giant duelling robot built onto the bow of the blockade's lead battleship, so the ship is its lower half and its rapier is the length of a frigate. - **Sample lines:**
  - *"A courier! In a stolen titan! At my blockade! Darling, you've made my whole decade. En garde."*
  - *"Nobody crosses the Rim. I say it kindly. I say it beautifully. I say it every single time."*

He loses the tower, then loses his stripe, then spends Acts 1 and 2 turning up at a lower rank each time, filing complaints about Red that nobody reads. In Act 3 Vela hands him an enlistment form, in triplicate, and he signs on with the team-up because the real enemy is the biggest open line item in history and he is going to close it. The rebuilt Hushmaster finally gets aimed at something worth all eight legs, and after eleven years Kasp finally makes Lieutenant: Red promotes him with a thumbs-up, to command a unit of one, and it is the proudest moment of his life.
- **Sample lines:**

## Act 1 outline
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05

Beats 1–4 marked **[SLICE]** are the vertical slice: Harrow Landing, the Old Relay Tower, Sgt. Kasp, party Red/Otis/Mox, all on foot, about 30 minutes.

1. **[SLICE] Harrow Landing, night.** *Scale: on foot.* Red does her lamp check, grabs a courier job to the Old Relay Tower, and finds Sgt. Kasp's Signals crew shoving Otis's dockhands around. Before Otis can finish asking for help, Red is already swinging, and Otis joins in, apologizing to everyone he flattens.

At the foot of the mast Watch Zero's bell-ringing sit-in is driving Kasp's crew up the wall, and the old-timers show Red a back way in.

Kasp in the Hushmaster.** *Scale: on foot.* The crew flips the rig's eight legs out from under Kasp mid-spec-recital, and in the wreck Red finds a beacon part that plays the call clearly for the first time: it's her mom's voice (first clue of the reveal; see RESTRICTED section). 9. **Borrowing Biscuit.** *Scale: big robot.* Red "borrows" Otis's cargo walker Biscuit, Otis climbs in to make sure nobody scratches the paint, and the crew stomps through Harrow Landing while Watch Zero rings every bell and the Marchfolk drag containers into the street to block the troopers.
10. **Boss: Lt. Brunt in the *HNS Title Shot*.** *Scale: big robot.* In the floodlit cargo yard, with her troopers cheering from the stacks, Brunt calls every punch and still loses the bout. When the *HNS Title Shot* topples, Red swings Biscuit around to catch it before it lands on Brunt's own fans, and Brunt is thrilled: finally, a real opponent.
11. **Off Harrow.** *Scale: big robot.* Biscuit clambers into the hold of the last ore freighter as it lifts, and from the closing hatch Red watches every window on Harrow light up at once. On every screen in the Marches, Vane triples the bounty, and somewhere Tilly gasps with delight.

**Act goal:** Stop the jammer, then bust off a locked-down Harrow to go answer the call.
**Ends on:** Red leaves home for the first time, wanted across the whole Marches, and Vane is now taking her personally.
**Approx. length:** About 3 hours (the slice is about 30 minutes of it).

## THE TWIST (RESTRICTED: Creative Director and Writer only)
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (revised: the Keeper, beatdown finale)

_Other agents: stop reading here and skip to the Glossary._

**The cycle.** A thousand years ago a crew flew the last lane to see what lay past the dark. They never came back, but their voice did: *"We're past the last lane and can't get home. If anyone hears this, please come. Leave a light on for us."* Their families raised the relay masts to hear them (Relay Watch Zero descends from those families) and lit the first lamps. A crew answered, got caught too, and called for help in the only words they knew: the call they had followed. It has repeated ever since. Every crew that reaches the heart of the Quiet becomes the next crew's call, with the newest voice on top and the older ones stacked faintly beneath. That's why the words barely change, why old spacers remember a different voice, and why the beacon part plays Red's mom. The caught crews' empty ships pile up in a vast harbor. About thirty years ago it overflowed, and the oldest hulls drifted out to the edge where salvagers found them. Vane's navy, the prize titan (a long-ago crew climbed the same ladder) and the antique dish in Kasp's Hushmaster are all answerers' ships painted gray. The cycle runs on the best thing about the Marches: nobody leaves anybody out there.

**The real enemy: the Keeper.** A god in the gap between galaxies. The Quiet is its hand, cupped around this edge of the galaxy. Its fingers are the dark, which is why there are no stars and radios hiss. In its palm burns a warm amber light like a lamp in a window, ringed by the harbor. That light is every star it has ever taken, squeezed into one glow, and it's bait. It keeps everything that reaches it (starlight, sound, ships, people) because it has never let go of anything, ever. Its grip only sets when a caught crew calls for help, and every crew does. They sleep, unaged, in amber beads strung along its mooring lines, and their call is the one thing it lets out, because each call fetches another crew. It never says anything original, only scraps of borrowed calls (Vane, appalled: *"It doesn't even write its own material!"*). Fighting it is pure spectacle: dark fingers closing on the fleet, knuckles armored in a thousand years of hulls, a voice made of every crew at once. **How it's beaten:** while its grip holds, it can't really be hurt: every dent gets patched with another hull reeled in from the harbor, and the stacked call drowns out the fleet. Once the grip breaks (Act 3, beat 10), it has five things to hit, one per scale: the mooring lines (on foot), the finger joints (big robot), the knuckle armor (giant robot), the palm (giant spaceship) and, last, the fist it closes around its light (god). *Name: Ross chose the Keeper.*

**The reveal (Act 2, beat 12).** The *HNS Magnificent* breaks apart, and the heat strips its gray paint. Under it, every salvaged hull wears a hand-painted lamp and a name nobody knows. All their old beacons switch on at once: the call, in a hundred voices and accents, saying the same words. The racket wakes the Keeper. The dark uncurls like an opening hand onto the harbor of empty ships from every century, and amber lines reel the wreck's salvaged hulls home, leaving behind only the part Vane built (he takes this personally). Vela's notes and Watch Zero's records unscramble the *Supper's On*'s log: Red's mom was answering the crew before hers, Ruo's brother's, and her last entry is the call itself, in her voice. Ruo spots his brother's ship in the harbor. Then the light turns toward them and speaks in every voice at once. Mox panics for a lot longer than four seconds. Red gives the dark a thumbs-up. Cut to black. It's a huge turn because everything flips at once: the call was the trap, Mom was an answerer, not the caller, Red's journey is a rerun, the Navy is built from heroes' ships, and the villain was only the opening act.

**Red's mom, Eurydice "Dee" Kincaid,** is the newest bead, nearest the light, asleep and not a day older. In beat 10 she's the first to wake. She sees her jacket on her kid (*"That's my jacket. Fits you terribly. Keep it."*), takes the helm of her own ship, and comes home for lights out.

**Red's mom's name** (Ross's pick, 2026-10-05): **Eurydice "Dee" Kincaid.** The nod: Orpheus goes down into the underworld to bring Eurydice back and loses her by looking back. Red goes into the dark for her mom and never looks back; she flies deeper. Everyone calls her Dee; only the call's log and Vane (being insufferable) use "Eurydice."

**Red's choice and the beatdown (Act 3, beat 10).** The Keeper's fingers close on the *Supper's On*. This is the moment when every crew before called for help. The ship's beacon (the slice's beacon part, fitted by Mox in beat 3) switches on by itself and the channel opens. Red switches it off. Goggles down, thumbs-up to the crew, and she flies deeper instead of out, with the whole fleet behind her on Vane's fanfare. With no call, the Keeper's grip can't set, and for the first time in a thousand years it's holding something it can't keep. She reaches Dee, and a crew that's reached wakes up. Each woken crew turns to the crew it originally came out to save, all the way back through a thousand years in seconds, until every rescue is finally finished. No new call goes out, every old one is answered, and every crew in history is awake, and angry. That isn't the ending. That's the opening bell.

- **Phase 1: the mooring lines.** *Scale: on foot.* The Keeper tries to reel the waking beads back into its palm. The party, Brunt's Hardfall Corps and Tilly sprint the amber lines on foot, cutting beads loose before they're hauled in, while freshly woken crews stumble out and grab the nearest wrench. Tilly's net cannon finally catches something worth catching.
- **Phase 2: the fingers.** *Scale: big robot.* Biscuit and the patched *HNS Title Shot* pry the fingers back one joint at a time. Brunt calls her shots on a god ("Left hook! The finger! Next turn!") and doesn't miss, and Otis apologizes to it first: *"Sorry about this, friend. Nothing personal. Mostly structural."*
- **Phase 3: the knuckles.** *Scale: giant robot.* The *Second Helping*, Calloway's rebuilt *Second Draft* and Sorrell's *HNS Riposte* crack the knuckle armor, and the armor switches sides: every woken crew flies its own ship off the Keeper's knuckles and turns its guns around. Calloway labels the god mid-swing ("OUCH. PROMISING.").
- **Phase 4: the palm.** *Scale: giant spaceship.* Dee takes the helm of the *Supper's On* and leads everything that flies into the palm: Vane's fleet, the Marches fleet and a thousand years of reclaimed ships, all moving on Vane's fanfare. Kasp kicks the Hushmaster back online and jams the Keeper's last borrowed voices so it can't fake a call, and the fleet's broadsides roll ship to ship down the whole line. Ruo and Tilly fly cold between its fingers, scoring each other the whole way.
- **Phase 5: the god.** *Scale: intergalactic god. The finishing sequence, several turns long.* The Keeper drags the rest of itself out of the gap between galaxies and clenches its hand into a fist around its light, because it has never let go of anything. In a cutscene, the first crew's strength passes down a thousand years of crews, each crew giving it to the next, until it reaches Dee, and Dee passes it on to her kid with every crew in history behind it. Red goes god-sized: Red herself, up-ear standing as tall as it goes, Mom's jacket the size of a nebula and still three sizes too big, the brass lamp swinging on its chain like a sun. It's a full god-against-god slugfest, not a single turn. Every crew in history is fighting behind her, and every friend's signature move comes back at god scale: Heave-Ho on a god, Approved in Triplicate stamped across the sky, Ten Out of Ten fired in star clusters, and one Patent Pending with a truly wild result. Vane, looking up at her: *"That was supposed to be my statue."* The final blow is Red's: Porch Light at god scale, a flare that lights the whole Quiet at once, then one last timed press, the biggest in the game, for the full-spin sword wallop that smashes the fist open.

**The Keeper's fate.** Its hand breaks. The fingers crack off and tumble into the harbor as a thousand years of salvage, the amber light in its palm shatters like lamp glass, and every star it ever took pours out at once. It opens its mouth for a last word and has none: every voice it ever borrowed has woken up and gone home. (Vane: *"Not even a closing line."*) Beaten, broken and empty-handed, what's left of it sinks back into the gap between galaxies, conquered. Tilly finally sticks her little price tag on the biggest piece of knuckle.

**Red becomes God.** When the light settles, Red is still god-sized, and the power isn't going anywhere: every crew in history handed it to her, and not one of them wants it back. She's the biggest thing in two galaxies. Her first act as God: she gathers the starlight pouring out of the broken hand and throws it, fistful by fistful, hanging every stolen star back in its place until the Quiet is the brightest sky in the Marches and the compasses point home again. Then she shrinks back to regular size, because the *Supper's On* has a seat with her name on it, and the power comes with her: it settles into the brass lamp on her belt, a little star burning inside the glass. She could be God again any time she likes. In beat 12 she lights that lamp one last time, taps the glass twice and blows it out anyway, because Dee's home and it's lights out. In the very last frame, if you look closely, something under the glass is still glowing.

**Unspoken.** Nobody ever names what this is about. The images carry it: sleeping and waking, a dark ship lit again, a mother no older than the day she left, the dark breaking into stars, and lamps blown out.
