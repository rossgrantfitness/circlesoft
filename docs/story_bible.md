## Logline
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (tone pass)

Ten years ago, scrap courier Pell Arden's mom roared off into the starless Quiet to answer a distress call nobody else had the guts to touch. Now the Hegemony wants that call shut up for good, so Pell is going to answer it herself, and if an entire empire wants to stand in her way, that's honestly the empire's problem.

## World & history
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (tone pass)

**The Outer Marches.** The ragged edge of the galaxy, where the stars thin out and the people get tougher. Dust moons, ring stations, rowdy trading ports. Everybody's broke, everything's held together with tape, and anybody will lend you a wrench. Harrow, Pell's home, is one dust moon out of hundreds, and by local accounts the loudest.

**Jump lanes.** Invisible highways that cross days of space in hours. Flying off-lane is slow and a terrible idea. Pilots hand down lane charts like secret family chili recipes. Whoever holds the lanes holds the Marches. The last lane ends at the Rim.

**The Hegemony.** The empire running the Marches from the Yards, a shipyard world wrapped in docks that never stop building. It brings roads, order, taxes and a truly heroic amount of paperwork, and backs it all with a navy. Its admirals give speeches. Its grunts mostly want lunch.

**The Quiet and the call.** Past the Rim lies the Quiet: a starless dark where compasses spin and radios hiss. Ships go in; none have come out. Yet. Out of it comes the call, a distress signal any cheap receiver can catch, asking for help in plain words, nonstop, forever. Old spacers swear it sounded different when they were young; everyone else rolls their eyes. The Hegemony made answering it a crime "for safety," which instantly made it the coolest crime on the frontier.

**The lamp in the window.** Families with someone out in the Quiet light a lamp in the window every night until that someone comes home. Nobody's ever gotten to blow one out. Pell plans to be first.

**Where fantasy lives.** Sci-fi is the hardware; fantasy is the culture. Spacers wear lucky charms, belt out lane-songs through every jump, swear by deck-side fortune readers, and trade Quiet ghost stories at the bar. The relay masts on the dust moons, older than any records, are half ruin, half shrine. Everyone is a chibi animal and nobody ever mentions it: species is never a joke, a trait, a power or a plot point.

**What the public knows (timeline)**
- **Before records:** The lanes, the relay masts, the call and the lamp custom are already there. Nobody knows who started any of it.
- **About 200 years ago:** The Hegemony rolls out from the Yards and plants its flag on the Marches.
- **About 30 years ago:** Salvage crews start hauling derelicts off the edge of the Quiet: hulls of every make and age, some no shipyard recognizes. A rising officer named Sarrow Vane bolts the modern navy together out of them and paints the whole mess gray.
- **About 25 years ago:** The Answering Ban. Crossing the Rim to answer the call becomes a crime.
- **10 years ago:** Pell's mom, Harrow's hottest pilot, defies the ban and blasts off into the Quiet. Harrow still tells the story, and it gets bigger every time.
- **Now:** Hegemony crews start bolting jammers onto the old relay masts, starting with Harrow's. Big mistake.

## Factions
> ✅ APPROVED by Ross, 2026-10-05 (tone pass)

| Faction | What they want | Look / symbol | Role in story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hegemony Navy** (Admiral Sarrow Vane's fleet) | Every jump lane, total order on the frontier, and the Answering Ban enforced to the letter. Vane wants more: whatever lies at the heart of the Quiet, for his fleet, and ideally a very large statue of himself next to it. | Slate-gray armor with white trim and numbered helmets (roll call is a disaster; nobody remembers their number). Vane's officers wear white greatcoats, and Vane keeps a junior officer whose whole job is making his coat billow on cue. The ships are mismatched hulls of every shape, all painted the same gray. Symbol: a white ring cut by one straight bar (a slipway seen from above). | The big bad wall of gray across all three acts: lands troops on Harrow in Act 1, holds the Rim blockade in Act 2, chases the party into the Quiet in Act 3. Vane is a gloriously theatrical villain: monologues, dramatic entrances, a fanfare when he walks into a room. His grunts are lovable punching bags who grumble about overtime and wave a white flag the second a fight goes sideways, so beating them up is pure fun. Where those salvaged hulls came from matters (see RESTRICTED section). |
| **The Signals Corps** (Hegemony) | Perfectly quiet airwaves: every relay mast jammed, the call silenced for good, and every form filed in triplicate. | Pressed blue-gray uniforms, oversized headphones, antenna pins on the collar, a clipboard always within reach. Symbol: a wavy line with a slash through it (a cut signal). Tradition: they write noise tickets for everything (engines, wind chimes, the call itself) and are very proud of their quotas. | The Hegemony's fussiest department. In the slice, Sergeant Kasp, a blustering middle manager who will not stop bragging about his spider-legged jammer rig, is fitting the Old Relay Tower. Wren Tallow is one of theirs; she has to listen to the call to jam it, actually listens, and at the end of Act 1 quits in style, handing over her resignation form, in triplicate, mid-battle. It's the first big crack in the Hegemony. |
| **The Marchfolk** (frontier moon folk) | To be left alone: fair prices, free lanes, their customs kept, and their people home safe. | Patched work clothes, dust scarves, goggles, lucky charms on every belt and bulkhead. Symbol: the lamp in the window. Tradition: every argument on the frontier gets settled with a bet, and the bets get bigger the worse the odds. | The people the game is about, and the rowdiest crowd in the galaxy. Harrow Landing is the slice town. At first most keep their heads down, since the Hegemony signs the paychecks, but they're itching for someone to give them a reason, and a few already slip Pell supplies on the sly. In Act 2 they come over moon by moon as Pell's legend grows bigger (and wildly more exaggerated) with every retelling. |
| **The Coldrunners** (smugglers and lane-runners) | Profit, unpatrolled routes, and their family lane charts, including secret side-lanes no Hegemony map shows. | Dark coats, running lights painted over, ships that fly "cold" with engines low to slip past patrols. Symbol: a shuttered lantern, chalked on crates and hatches. Tradition: they score each other's getaways for style, and a boring escape is the worst insult there is. | The outlaw way around the Hegemony. Pell's courier jobs brush up against their crates in the slice. In Act 1 they're the only way off a locked-down Harrow, and they might help her, sell her out, or try both at once. In Act 2, Ruo Vantell and his crew give the party a ship to the Rim. Some Coldrunners will fly an answerer rimward, for a steep price and bonus points for flair. |
| **The Tenders** (keepers of the relay masts) | The old masts left standing and unjammed, the call heard, and the old customs kept: lane-songs, charms, lighting the lamps. | Dust-colored wraps, little bells sewn to their sleeves, big brass ear-cups for listening. Symbol: a mast with three rings around its top. Tradition: they protest by ringing their sleeve bells all at once, and they hold the frontier record for noise tickets from the Signals Corps. | A small order of cranky, fearless old-timers who treat the half-ruined masts like shrines and have never once lost a shouting match. In the slice, Harrow's Tenders stage the loudest sit-in in Marches history against the jammer and show Pell a back way into the Old Relay Tower. They keep hand-copied records of the call going back further than anyone else's (see RESTRICTED section). |

In the vertical slice, the Signals Corps is jamming Harrow's mast, the Tenders are bell-ringing it into a headache, most Marchfolk are placing bets on who cracks first, and the Coldrunners are selling snacks to both sides. Pell is right in the middle: a courier who runs the odd Coldrunner crate, lights a lamp the Tenders would bless, and is already picking a fight with Kasp. In Act 1 the Navy locks Harrow down with maximum fanfare, which knocks the Marchfolk off the fence, leaves the Coldrunners deciding whether Pell is worth more as a bounty or a passenger, and cracks the Signals Corps wide open when Wren stops jamming the call and starts listening to it.

## Main cast
> 🟡 DRAFT: awaiting Ross's approval

Pell, Osric and Mox are the vertical slice party. Wren joins at the end of Act 1; Ruo joins in Act 2.

### Pell Arden
- **Role:** Hero, and the party's quick front-line fighter. A scrap courier on Harrow who has lit her mom's window lamp every single night for ten years. Playable from the first second of the game (slice party).
- **Look:** Chibi fennec fox with enormous ears and sandy fur. Wears her mom's beat-up red flight jacket, three sizes too big, sleeves rolled into fat cuffs, with Harrow patches and a hand-painted lamp on the back. Goggles pushed up between the ears, courier satchel, scuffed boots. Signature item: a heavy brass hand lamp on a short chain clipped to her belt, which she swings like a flail. Silhouette: giant ears, giant jacket, little lamp swinging at her hip.
- **Personality:** Stubborn hope with a sharp tongue: she would rather be wrong and moving than right and waiting. Quirk: every time she hears a new, wildly exaggerated version of her own legend, she loudly corrects it, then adds an even bigger exaggeration of her own. Under the noise she is the most loyal person on the frontier, and if somebody asks for help she has already started running.
- **Battle style:** Fast hits and lamp-swing combos, great at starting a Relay chain; signature move **Porch Light**, a blinding lamp flare followed by a full-spin wallop.
- **Arc:** Pell sets out answering one call for one person, and over the journey ends up fighting for every lamp-lit window in the Marches. She learns that hope isn't just refusing to quit; it's letting people come along with you. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"You brought a jammer, a navy and a permit. I brought a lamp and a bad attitude. Let's see who's overdressed."*
  - *"Somebody called for help. I don't need a better reason than that."*

### Osric Dunmore
- **Role:** Tank, and later the crew's walker pilot. Harrow's dock boss and the closest thing the docks have to a dad. Joins in the slice when Sergeant Kasp's crew starts shoving his dockhands around (slice party). At the end of Act 1, Pell "borrows" his cargo walker, Biscuit, to bust off Harrow, and Osric comes along to make sure nobody scratches the paint.
- **Look:** Chibi walrus, big and round, with polished brass caps on his tusks and a bushy mustache. Faded orange dockworker coveralls, a padded work vest, a hard hat with a little headlamp, and thick work gloves. Signature item: a dented cargo-container door with a handle welded on, used as a shield. Silhouette: a friendly boulder carrying a door.
- **Personality:** The gentlest soul on Harrow; he hums old work songs under his breath and never, ever raises his voice, even while lifting a trooper over his head. Quirk: he politely apologizes to every enemy before he flattens them, and tucks a cough drop in the pocket of anyone he knocks out. He used to pilot for the Navy and walked off one day without explanation; at heart he believes every crew deserves someone waiting for them on the dock.
- **Battle style:** Soaks hits, shields allies to protect Relay chains from breaking; signature move **Heave-Ho**, picking up one enemy and setting it down, very firmly, on another.
- **Arc:** Osric has spent years keeping his head down and his dock quiet, and the journey asks him to stand up and get loud for something. He raises his voice exactly once in the whole game, and it should rattle the speakers. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Sorry about this, friend. Nothing personal. Mostly structural."*
  - *"A dock is just a long wait with a roof on it. Somebody has to stand at the end of it."*

### Mox
- **Role:** Mechanic and gadget fighter; the crew's engineer. A Harrow dock kid who joins in the slice by stowing away in Pell's delivery crate on the run to the Old Relay Tower (slice party). Pell tries to send him home several times. It never works.
- **Look:** Chibi ferret kid, long and wiry, with a welding mask flipped up on his head like a crown. An oversized utility vest with far too many pockets, each one bulging with something, plus a wrench nearly as tall as he is. A small, wobbly homemade drone named Tuesday (built on a Tuesday) buzzes over his shoulder. Every gadget he builds is welded with "MOX WUZ HERE." Silhouette: mask, wrench, pockets, drone.
- **Personality:** Brags nonstop about inventions that are about forty percent finished. Quirk: he panics exactly once per crisis, loudly, for about four seconds, then gets to work, so the crew has learned to just wait it out. Underneath the boasting is a kid who has never been off Harrow and is terrified of being left behind, which is exactly why he keeps climbing into crates.
- **Battle style:** Pulls random gadgets out of random pockets: traps, buffs, repairs and the occasional small explosion; signature move **Patent Pending**, an untested contraption with a wild (and occasionally amazing) result.
- **Arc:** Mox starts as a stowaway chasing glory and grows into the engineer who keeps every walker, titan and ship running when it matters most. Along the way he finds out the crew was never going to leave him behind, and he stops bragging about what he'll build and starts building it.
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Stand back, everybody, I've done this a hundred times! ...Once. I've watched somebody do it once."*
  - *"You can tell me to go home. I'm just gonna get in the next crate."*

### Wren Tallow
- **Role:** Healer and support. A Signals Corps specialist assigned to jam the call, who has to listen to it to do her job, actually listens, and at the end of Act 1 quits in style mid-battle by handing over her resignation form, in triplicate, then switching sides on the spot.
- **Look:** Chibi heron, tall and long-legged for a chibi, with a sleek gray-white crest. Pressed blue-gray Signals Corps uniform (she keeps it pressed even after she quits, with the Corps patch swapped for a hand-stitched lamp), oversized headphones around her neck, an earpiece always in, and an antenna pin on the collar. Signature items: a clipboard and a rubber stamp she wields like a holy relic. Silhouette: tall, upright, clipboard held like a shield.
- **Personality:** Prim, exact, and speaks seven languages, none of them casually. Quirk: she still writes noise tickets out of pure habit, including for Osric's snoring, Mox's explosions and her own heroic war cries. At her core she is someone who heard a stranger asking for help, and could not unhear it, and that was the end of her career.
- **Battle style:** Heals, shields and tidies up Relay chains so they don't drop; signature move **Approved in Triplicate**, three loud stamps that patch up the whole party.
- **Arc:** Wren begins certain that following the rules is the same thing as doing good, and breaks the biggest rule on the frontier because listening was the right thing to do. Her careful notes on the call become one of the crew's most important tools. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"Under regulation 14-C, I'm required to warn you before I do this. Consider yourself warned. Twice. Once more for my records."*
  - *"They told me to listen so I'd know what to silence. I listened. Somebody needs help. That's the whole message."*

### Ruo Vantell
- **Role:** Fast attacker and thief; Coldrunner captain and the crew's ship. Joins in Act 2 when the party needs a ride to the Rim, flying his ship, the *Low Profile* (the loudest ship ever to fly cold).
- **Look:** Chibi raccoon with a dramatic striped tail and a mask that looks like he was born for this job. A long dark Coldrunner coat with the running-light patches painted over, a scarf he flips over his shoulder at every opportunity, and fingerless gloves. Signature items: a shuttered lantern on his belt (the Coldrunner symbol) and twin flare pistols. Silhouette: long coat, big tail, a lantern swinging behind him.
- **Personality:** Runs blockades, flirts with danger, and has a reputation even worse than his manners. Quirk: true to Coldrunner tradition, he scores everything out loud for style, from getaways to the crew's breakfast to his own entrances (always a ten). His big brother flew rimward to answer the call eleven years ago, and Ruo keeps a lamp lit in his cockpit that he swears is just for reading.
- **Battle style:** Quick strikes, item steals and showy finishers that cash in long Relay chains; signature move **Ten Out of Ten**, a flare-pistol volley that gets more dazzling the longer the chain before it.
- **Arc:** Ruo signs on claiming it's strictly for the fee, and spends Act 2 slowly admitting he was always going to the Rim anyway. The smuggler who has spent his whole life finding ways around things finally decides to go straight through. (twist-related beats: see RESTRICTED section)
- **Sample lines:**
  - *"That was a six. Sloppy landing, no flair. Watch me, I'll show you a ten."*
  - *"I've been flying the long way around the Rim for eleven years. Turns out I was just waiting for somebody stubborn enough to go straight."*
