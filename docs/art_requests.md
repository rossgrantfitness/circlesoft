**Vertical slice art list (42 assets: 41 approved 2026-10-06, plus the train-car kit, row 58, approved with the structure pass 2026-10-08).** The look: PSX style, Mega Man Legends, Tail Concerto, MGS1, FF7, **in the grim, slum-cyberpunk look Ross approved on 2026-10-08** (desaturated, darker, grimy; characters and enemies a little brighter than the world, with an edge light). All poly and texture numbers below are **starting budgets; final numbers set by the style guide**.

**Updated 2026-10-08:** rows 17, 19, 20, 21, 37, 47 and 50 follow the approved tone and structure passes (slum Harrow, the Spillway, the jammer works, the train inspectors, spray-paint stencil ratings); rows 56 (grim dressing kit) and 57 (grim Red) are now approved, not conditional; row 58 (train-car kit) is new.

**Priority follows the build order:** P1 = party and weapons (battle, M2; Red first) · P2 = Harrow Landing: portraits, townsfolk, town sets (M3) · P3 = the ore train, the Spillway and the jammer works: sets, enemies, props (M4) · P4 = the boss (M5) · P5 = UI (M7).

**Portraits: 24 images in 6 sets** (Red, Otis, Mox × 5 expressions = 15; Kasp + 2 key NPCs × 3 = 9). Crowd NPCs get none.

**Model contract (2026-10-06):** if a character model is outsourced, it must follow the "Model contract" in docs/style_guide.md: six clips named `idle`, `walk`, `run`, `jump`, `fall`, `land` (15 fps stepped), facing +Z in Godot (-Y in Blender), 1 unit = 1 m with Red 1.0 tall, origin at the feet, body at most 900 tris, 128x128 body texture plus 128x64 face sheet. The placeholder shiba Red (game/art/placeholder/characters/red/red_shiba.glb) is the working example, and Red's look is locked to prototype F (red_proto_f.glb). Gesture clips are on hold.

**Shared notes:** 3D models as .glb, textures as .png, nearest-neighbor, low color count (per the style guide). **Characters are animals in the approved grim look (2026-10-08):** taller and leaner than the old chibi, smaller heads, matte dusty fur with no gloss spots, faded, scuffed and stained clothes (see the story bible's Look lines and the style guide's Character proportions). The **edge light** is a shader effect done in code: don't paint rim light into textures. Paint characters a notch lighter in value than the environments they stand in ("brighter, not too bright"), and keep every texture dirty and muted before the grim screen grade. Weapons are separate props held in the hand, so models need a hand attach point. Animation lists come from the Technical Artist with the tech plan. Final art goes under game/art/final/ (exact folders confirmed by the tech plan); agents' placeholders stay in game/art/placeholder/.

## Vertical slice (2026-10-09)

Task VS-2 (Creative Director). The art for the action vertical slice Ross approved on 2026-10-09 (docs/decisions.md): the night market, the robot junkyard, Kasp in the Hushmaster on foot, then Kasp's giant junk mech against Red's loader docked into the colossus, and four hacks. Each row ID matches the task board (VS-A1 to VS-A7); priority runs in that order. Blockouts and placeholders stand in until each drop arrives (VS-24 for the bosses), so nothing waits on Ross.

**Sizes and budgets are starting numbers.** The Technical Artist confirms sizes against the blockouts (VS-24) and the arena map (VS-13). **Budgets are style guides, not hard caps** ("look, not limits", Ross 2026-10-08): going over to keep a shape readable is fine, and so is coming in under if it reads.

**Made with Meshy AI plus Ross's own textures. What works well:**
- **One mesh per file is fine.** Parts that break off or move on their own (Hushmaster legs, relay boxes and dish; the junk mech's plates and core; the turret head; the crane hook; drone rotors) work best as their **own small files**, because Meshy fuses everything into one shell and a cut leaves holes. How Ross sends them is a decision on the summary page.
- **Anything that walks on Red's skeleton** (Kasp, the heavy, Otis, Mox, Vela, market residents, the junk mech): **A-pose** (T-pose also works), **unrigged**. The Animator/Rigger rigs to our bone names (docs/pivot/combat_api.md section 5) and retargets the free Quaternius clips. If Meshy's auto-rig is used, send the unrigged file too.
- **PBR textures are fine** (base colour, normal, roughness and metal, as Meshy exports them; JPEG or PNG). Ross's own textures can replace the base colour at any time. The Technical Artist handles import, filtering, the grade and the edge light.
- **Scale and facing:** model to real height in metres if Meshy allows it; otherwise we scale on import from the height in the row. Front faces +Z in Godot; we fix origin and facing on import.
- **Polycount:** set Meshy's target polycount or remesh near the row's number. Red came in at 1,614 tris and looks right; that is the scale of detail to aim for.
- **Don't paint rim light**: the edge light is code. Characters sit a notch brighter than the place they stand in ("brighter, not too bright").

**Colour language for the slice:** **Signals** things are blue-gray #6F8BA8 and slate #5B6573 with white stencils and cold pink-red status lamps #E8456A. **Cyan means Red's hacking**: hijacked machines turn cyan in code and Red's jacket strips are cyan and orange, so keep cyan off Signals machines. **The market** is warm: amber, sodium orange, a hot pink and a dull teal neon, string lights, faded tarps. **The junkyard** is rust, oil and faded car paint under a grim sky. The light grim grade (Ross, 2026-10-08, B) goes over Ross's colours; it doesn't drain them.

**Originality:** the robots and machines are **original designs: no Gurren Lagann look** (no face on the chest, no drill, no sunglasses, no spiral motifs), and the junk mech has **no face at all**. Docking is the one borrowed beat (Ross, 2026-10-09). Everyone else gets the stacking test: one borrowed role is fine, a match on role, look, catchphrase and plot all at once is not.

**Older rows:** these rows are the slice versions of rows 2, 3, 22, 33 and 34 and of the town and works rows. The old rows stay as the record (nothing deleted); new work follows these.
